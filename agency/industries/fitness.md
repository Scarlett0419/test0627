# Industry Playbook: Fitness

**Covers:** independent gyms · CrossFit boxes · martial arts / boxing / MMA · yoga & pilates studios · personal-training (PT) studios
**Use with:** `OFFER.md`, `SETTER_SCRIPT.md`, `CLOSER_SCRIPT.md`, `DEMO_PLAYBOOK.md`, `.claude/skills/vertical-site-conventions/references/shape-local-service-booking.md`

> **No-stats rule:** This playbook has no market figures in it on purpose. Everything here is a qualitative rule of thumb. If you add a number, cite a source you actually checked. Only use facts on calls that you verified about *that* gym.

---

## 1. How they get customers today

**Where their customers come from**
- **Referrals and community.** Members bring friends, partners and kids. The "family" feel is the product.
- **Facebook/Instagram.** Class clips, PR boards, fight results, member spotlights. A Facebook page often acts as the whole website.
- **Google Maps.** "Gym near me," "BJJ near me," "kids karate [city]."
- **Software** (Mindbody, Wodify, PushPress, Zen Planner, Glofox, Spark, Kicksite, Arketa, Momence, Mariana Tek). The public schedule page is used as the site.
- **Local presence:** tournaments, 5Ks, school events, corporate/first-responder deals, Groupon/ClassPass (studios).
- Martial arts: kids' programs and parents are a huge driver. Parents *research* before they visit.

**Why so many have no website**
- The coach/owner runs classes early mornings and evenings. Marketing gets whatever time is left.
- "Our Facebook is where everyone is."
- Past sites expired or the domain lapsed after a designer left.
- Garage-gym roots: it started as a community of friends, then grew.
- They think the gym software's schedule page counts as a site.

**Decision-maker**
| Sub-type | Who decides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Independent gym | Owner, sometimes owner + spouse/partner | May have a front-desk person during peak hours |
| CrossFit box | Owner/head coach | Very community-driven. May ask "what do the members think" |
| Martial arts / boxing | Head instructor/owner ("Coach," "Sensei," "Professor") | Use the title they use. Parents are the buyers of kids' programs |
| Yoga / pilates | Studio owner (often also teaches) | Design-sensitive. Cares about vibe |
| PT studio | Owner-trainer | Sells high-ticket packages. Values lead quality |

**When to call** (check their class schedule)
- **Best:** late morning to early afternoon (roughly 10am–2pm) on weekdays, between the morning and evening class blocks. That's when owners do admin.
- **Worst:** 5–8am and 4–8pm (peak classes), Saturday morning classes, and during tournaments or competitions (check their FB).
- Martial arts schools often open mid-afternoon. Call just before the kids' classes start, or late morning if a cell is listed.
- If they're coaching: "When's your break between classes?" Then call back exactly then.

## 2. What the website must do

**Must-haves**
1. **Free trial / intro offer CTA** in the header and hero: "Book a Free Class," "Claim Your Free Week," "Book a No-Sweat Intro." This is *the* conversion action. Only show an offer the gym really runs. Confirm it on the call.
2. **Class schedule**, embedded from their software (Mindbody/Wodify/PushPress etc.) or as a simple table.
3. **Programs pages:** e.g. Adult BJJ / Kids Martial Arts / Boxing Fitness; or CrossFit / Olympic Lifting / Open Gym; or Vinyasa / Hot / Reformer; or 1:1 / Small Group PT.
4. **Pricing or "memberships start at."** Many gyms hide pricing. Let the owner decide, but at minimum explain the options and the intro path.
5. **Coaches/instructors** with photos, credentials (belt rank, certifications), and bios.
6. **Gallery/video** of real classes and the space. Stock "fitness models" hurt trust.
7. **Reviews/transformations.** Real, with permission. No made-up before/afters.
8. **Click-to-call, map, parking, "what to bring / first visit" FAQ.** First-timers are nervous.
9. **Kids' program page** with parent-focused copy (safety, discipline, age groups) for martial arts.

**Tier fit** (see `OFFER.md`)
| Sub-type | Tier |
|---|---|
| Solo PT, small single-program yoga studio | **Foundation** |
| Independent gym, CrossFit box, martial arts school, multi-class studio | **Full Presence** (the default for gyms per OFFER.md): schedule, programs, trial booking, coaches, gallery |

## 3. Owner language and pains

**Words they use:** "members," "drop-ins," "trial," "intro," "leads," "churn," "retention," "the community," "the box," "the mat," "coaches," "on-ramp," "belt test," "January rush," "summer slump," "ClassPass."

**Real pains**
- Trials that don't show up, and leads coming through FB DMs at 11pm.
- Churn. Summer and holiday dips.
- New big-box or franchise gyms opening nearby with slick sites and low prices.
- Coaching all day, then answering messages at night.
- Parents can't find kids' class info, so they call during class.
- Being seen as "just a garage gym" when they're a serious program.
- Depending on ClassPass/Groupon, which bring low-value one-timers.

**SPIN questions for the closer**
- **P:** "How do new people usually book their first class right now?"
- **I:** "When a lead messages at night and you reply the next afternoon, how often do they still show up?"
- **N:** "If they could book a trial class straight from Google in 30 seconds, what would that do for your intro numbers?"
- **P:** "What happens when the new [franchise gym] down the road shows up on Google next to you?"
- **I:** "If someone compares a polished site with a Facebook page, who do they assume is the better gym, even if it's not true?"
- **N:** "What would it be worth to look online like the gym you really are?"
- **P (martial arts):** "How do parents find out about the kids' program, ages and times?"
- **I:** "How many calls does that mean during class, and how many just never call back?"
- **N:** "Would a kids' page that answers the parent's questions and books a trial help fill the next session?"
- **P:** "How much of your new business comes from ClassPass or Groupon?"
- **I:** "How many of those turn into full members vs. one-and-done?"
- **N:** "Would having your own intro offer that people book directly change your margin?"
- **P (PT studio):** "How do you screen leads before a consult?"
- **N:** "Would a site that qualifies them first, with goals and budget and application-style booking, save you consult time?"

## 4. Objections and answers

**"We use Mindbody / Wodify / PushPress / Zen Planner."**
> "Great, keep it. The site plugs your schedule and signup straight into it. The software runs the gym. The website is what gets a stranger to trust you enough to book their first class. Right now Google sends them to a schedule page that looks like every other gym's."

**"Facebook/Instagram is enough."**
> "Your members are on Facebook, and that's why your community is strong. But new people search Google, and a lot of them aren't following you yet. The site catches them, then points to your socials to show the vibe. And it's yours if a platform changes the rules."

**"We're at capacity / full."**
> "Awesome, then it's about keeping it full and getting the right members. A site with clear programs and pricing screens out tire-kickers, supports a price increase, and gives you a waitlist or a second-location story later."

**"Members come from referrals."**
> "Best source there is. But when a member tells a friend about you, what does that friend do first? They Google you. The site is where the referral gets confirmed or lost."

**"We tried a website, it didn't do anything."**
> "Usually that's a site with no clear offer. Nice pictures, no 'Book a Free Class.' The demo is built around one action: trial booked. What did the old site ask people to do?"

**"Not now, January is coming / it's summer."** Use the timing in §7: "That's the reason. Twenty-one days from kickoff puts you live before the rush, not in the middle of it."

**"Price."** Use the CLOSER_SCRIPT handler. Anchor: "What's one member worth to you over a year? How many new members would it take to cover it?" Let them do the math. Never quote a number for them.

## 5. Setter openers and talking points

**Opener: gym / CrossFit**
> "Hey, is this [Name], the owner of [Gym]? [Name] from Vanguard. I'll be quick, I know you're probably between classes. I found [Gym] on Google, [verified: great reviews / open since 20XX], but there's no website, just the Facebook page. We built you a demo site with your programs and a 'Book a Free Class' button already on it. Can I get 15 minutes to walk you through it?"

**Opener: martial arts / boxing**
> "Hi, is this Coach [Name]? [Name] from Vanguard. I came across [Academy] while looking up [BJJ/kickboxing/karate] in [city]. You've got a great following, but parents searching for kids' classes can't find a website with ages and times. We built a demo that does exactly that. Worth a 15-minute look?"

**Opener: yoga / pilates**
> "Hi [Name], [Name] from Vanguard. I found [Studio] on Instagram, it looks like a beautiful space. There's no website, just the booking app, so we built a demo that shows your classes, teachers and intro offer in your style. Can I show you in 15 minutes?"

**Opener: PT studio**
> "Hi [Name], [Name] from Vanguard. Quick one, we built a demo website for [Studio] with a consult booking page that pre-qualifies people before they get to you. Open to a 15-minute walkthrough?"

**Talking points**
- "One button: book a free class/intro."
- "Plugs into your Mindbody/Wodify/PushPress, nothing to switch."
- "Lets you compete with the franchise gym on Google."
- "Parents get their answers without calling during class" (martial arts).
- "Live before January" (Sep–Dec calls) / "Live before summer" (Mar–Apr).
- Use their real title (Coach/Sensei/Professor) if it's public.

**Don'ts:** No promises of member counts. Don't mock the "garage gym." Don't call during class blocks.

## 6. Demo guidance (`scripts/generate_demo.py`)

- **Preset:** `--industry fitness` for every sub-type (default CTA "Start Training," hero "Get stronger…"). For yoga/pilates the preset's energy is too aggressive, so pass a calm `--tagline` (e.g. "Move, breathe, belong in [city]") and soften the hero during the manual polish in DEMO_PLAYBOOK. For kids' martial arts, the tagline should speak to parents ("Confidence and discipline for kids 4–12" *only if* those ages are verified).
- **`--services`:** their real programs, e.g. `"Adult BJJ,Kids BJJ,Muay Thai,Open Mat"` or `"CrossFit,Olympic Lifting,Open Gym,Personal Training"` or `"Vinyasa,Reformer Pilates,Private Sessions"`.
- **`--booking_url`:** their public trial or schedule link (Mindbody/Wodify/etc.) if one exists. Otherwise leave the default.
- **`--highlights`:** verified only:
  - `"4.9★:Google rating"`
  - `"Since 2018:Training [city]"` from their listing, FB "about," or local news
  - `"Black Belt:Head Instructor"` / `"CF-L2:Head Coach"` only if published by them or the certifying body
  - Never invent member counts, "pounds lost," win records, or testimonials.
- **Sections to emphasize:** hero with the free-trial CTA, programs, schedule (a placeholder labeled "Live schedule from [software] goes here" is fine), coaches, first-visit FAQ, gallery from their public socials, map + click-to-call. Add a kids' block for martial arts.
- **CTA wording:** "Book Your Free Class," "Claim Your Free Trial Week," "Book a No-Sweat Intro," "Try a Kids' Class Free," "Book a Free Consult" (PT). Always match the offer they actually run. If there's no trial, use "Book Your First Class."

## 7. Timing, budgets, red flags

**Seasonality (rule of thumb)**
- **New Year** (Jan) is the classic signup peak. The best time to sell a site is **Sep–Nov**, so it's live before it.
- **Pre-summer** (Mar–May) is the second push.
- **Back-to-school** (Aug–Sep) is key for kids' martial arts.
- Summer and Dec holidays are slower. Owners have more time to talk, but more budget anxiety.

**Budget / ability to pay**
- Established gyms, martial arts schools and PT studios with recurring memberships can usually pay upfront. They think in "member value," so use that framing.
- New or garage gyms and solo yoga teachers are tighter. Foundation fits, and upfront payment may take time. Don't improvise terms outside `OFFER.md`.
- Owners are used to paying monthly for software, so the Care Plan is an easy "yes" for them.

**Red flags (bad fit)**
- Gym is closing, for sale, or "might move soon" (check recent FB posts).
- A franchise location. Corporate usually controls the website, so disqualify politely.
- Wants guaranteed member numbers or pay-per-lead only.
- An online-only coach with no local location (a different offer).
- A partner dispute over who decides. Get both on the closer call or pass.
- Asks us to write health or weight-loss claims or fake transformations. We won't.

## 8. Upsells and Care Plan angles

- **Care Plan ($150–$250/mo):** schedule/program updates, holiday hours, seasonal challenge pages ("6-Week New Year Challenge"), coach roster changes, event and belt-test announcements, fresh photos. "Change your schedule, text us, done."
- **Seasonal landing pages:** New Year challenge, summer shred, back-to-school kids' enrollment. These fit naturally within Care Plan hours or as small add-ons.
- **Kids' program page and parent FAQ** (martial arts). A clear Full Presence justification.
- **Google Business Profile tune-up** with trial link, class categories and photos.
- **Lead capture and follow-up:** a trial form that feeds their software or email. Only include SMS follow-up with proper consent (see the `sms` skill / TCPA note in SETTER_SCRIPT).
- **Review routine:** a QR card at the front desk and a post-trial "leave us a review" link.
