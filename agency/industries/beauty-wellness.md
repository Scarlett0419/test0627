# Industry Playbook: Beauty & Wellness

**Covers:** barbershops · hair salons · nail salons · lash/brow studios · day spas · massage therapists
**Use with:** `OFFER.md` (tiers/pricing), `SETTER_SCRIPT.md`, `CLOSER_SCRIPT.md`, `DEMO_PLAYBOOK.md`, `.claude/skills/vertical-site-conventions/references/shape-local-service-booking.md`

> **No-stats rule:** This playbook has no market figures in it on purpose. Everything below is a qualitative rule of thumb. If you add a number, cite a source you actually checked. On calls, only use facts you verified about *that* business, like their Google rating or years open.

---

## 1. How they get customers today

**Where their customers come from**
- **Word of mouth and regulars.** This is the backbone. A good barber or stylist has a "book," meaning a base of clients who come back every 2–6 weeks.
- **Instagram/TikTok.** Before/after photos, reels of fades, nail sets and lash maps. The DM inbox often doubles as the booking desk.
- **Google Business Profile / Maps.** People search "barber near me" or "nail salon open now." Reviews and photos do the selling.
- **Booking apps** (Booksy, Vagaro, StyleSeat, Fresha, Square Appointments, GlossGenius, Schedulicity). Some add marketplace discovery, but the business's own profile page is mostly used as a link-in-bio.
- **Walk-ins.** Traditional barbershops and many nail salons are walk-in-heavy.
- Massage and spa also get gift-certificate buyers, couples, and hotel or tourist traffic.

**Why so many have no website**
- "Instagram is my website." The booking-app link feels like enough.
- Owners are craftspeople, not marketers. They're behind the chair all day.
- Past bad experiences: a nephew's Wix site, or an agency that vanished.
- Old-school shops (decades-old barbers, family nail salons) have run on walk-ins and loyalty forever.
- Chair/booth-rental shops: each renter markets themselves, so no one owns "the shop's" marketing.

**Decision-maker**
| Sub-type | Who decides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Barbershop | Owner-barber (often also cuts) | In booth-rent shops the owner is basically a landlord, so pitch the shop brand plus recruiting renters |
| Hair salon | Owner (often a senior stylist) | A front desk at bigger salons will gatekeep, so ask for the owner by name |
| Nail salon | Owner, sometimes a family member who handles the business side | Language barriers happen. Slow down, keep it simple, and offer to text the demo if they OK it |
| Lash/brow | Solo artist-owner | Fast decisions, small budgets |
| Spa | Owner or manager | Longer decision, and may involve a partner |
| Massage (LMT) | Solo therapist | Their phone is off during sessions, so expect voicemail |

**When to call** (check the listed hours first, since most are closed Sun and/or Mon)
- **Best:** right after opening (the first 15–30 min before the first client), mid-afternoon weekday lulls (roughly 2–4pm Tue–Thu), and their closed day *only if* the listing shows a personal cell. Many owners do admin on the closed day.
- **Worst:** Fri afternoon, all day Sat, the pre-holiday rush (prom, wedding season, Thanksgiving to New Year's), and lunch hour at walk-in barbershops.
- If they say "I'm with a client," don't pitch. Ask "When's your next break, 20 minutes or after 5?" and call back exactly then.

## 2. What the website must do

**Must-haves**
1. **Book Now button** in the header and hero. It should link to their *existing* booking app (Booksy/Vagaro/etc.). We don't replace it. For walk-in shops, use **Call / Get Directions** plus live hours instead.
2. **Service menu with prices and durations**, grouped (Cuts / Color / Nails / Lashes / Massage). "Starting at" pricing is fine.
3. **Gallery** of real work from their Instagram. This is the #1 trust asset.
4. **Team roster** with faces, names, specialties, and a personal booking link per stylist or barber. This is critical for booth-rent shops.
5. **Reviews** (real Google reviews embedded or linked, never invented).
6. **Click-to-call, map, hours, parking/entry notes.**
7. **Policies:** deposits, cancellations, late arrivals, and patch tests (lash/color). These cut no-shows and awkward DMs.
8. **Gift cards** (spa, massage, nails). This matters a lot in Q4.
9. New-client offer or first-visit info, if they run one.

**Tier fit** (see `OFFER.md`)
| Sub-type | Tier |
|---|---|
| Solo lash/brow, solo LMT, single-chair barber | **Foundation** ($1,800–$2,200) |
| Multi-chair barbershop, full salon, nail salon with a big menu | **Foundation**, or **Full Presence** if there's a roster plus gallery plus per-stylist booking |
| Day spa, multi-therapist massage, salon + spa | **Full Presence** ($2,400–$2,800): packages, gift cards, several service pages |

## 3. Owner language and pains

**Words they use:** "book," "books up," "chair," "booth rent," "no-shows," "last-minute cancels," "walk-ins," "regulars," "DMs," "link in bio," "slow Tuesdays," "gap in my schedule," "price shoppers," "deposit."

**Real pains**
- Answering the same DMs all day ("how much for a fade?", "do you do gel-x?") while working on a client.
- No-shows and cancellations. No clear place to post the policy.
- Instagram reach fell off, and posting constantly is tiring.
- Looking small or "not legit" next to the chain salon or the new studio down the street.
- Booth-rent turnover: it's hard to recruit good renters.
- The booking-app page looks like everyone else's, with the shop's brand buried under the platform's.
- Slow weekdays and empty gaps.

**SPIN questions for the closer**
- **P:** "How many DMs or calls a day are just people asking prices or availability?"
- **I:** "When you're mid-service and can't answer, what happens to that person? Do they wait, or book somewhere else?"
- **N:** "If your prices, policies and book button were in one link that answered those for you, what would that free up?"
- **P:** "How are no-shows and last-minute cancels this month?"
- **I:** "What does one empty hour cost you, and how many of those in a week?"
- **N:** "Would having your deposit and cancellation policy clearly posted before they book help?"
- **P:** "When someone new Googles '[niche] in [city],' what do they find for you vs. the shop down the street?"
- **I:** "If they see a full website for the other shop and just a listing for you, who do you think they trust first?"
- **N:** "How would it feel to send people a link that looks as good as your work?"
- **P (booth-rent):** "How hard is it to fill an open chair right now?"
- **I:** "How long does a chair sit empty, and what does that cost in rent?"
- **N:** "Would a 'Join the team / chair available' page that shows off the shop help you recruit?"
- **P (spa/massage):** "How do people buy gift cards from you today?"
- **N:** "What if they could buy one at 11pm on Dec 23rd without calling you?"

## 4. Objections and answers

**"I already use Booksy / Vagaro / StyleSeat / Fresha / Square."**
> "Perfect, keep it. We don't replace it. Your site's Book button goes straight into your Booksy. The app handles scheduling. The site is what makes a stranger on Google pick you and then click book. Right now Google shows them the app's page, which looks the same as every other shop on it."

**"Instagram is enough."**
> "Your Instagram is great, and that's why we pulled your photos into the demo. But Instagram only works for people who already follow you. Someone searching '[niche] near me' lands on Google, and Google ranks websites. It's also a place you own. If the algorithm or your account changes, the site still works."

**"I'm booked solid."**
> "Love that. So it's not about more clients, it's about better ones and fewer headaches. A site that shows your prices and deposit policy up front screens out price shoppers and no-shows, and can support a price increase. And if you ever add a chair or an assistant, it's ready to fill them."

**"It's a chair-rental shop, the barbers do their own marketing."**
> "Right, and that's the angle. The site gives every barber their own card and book link, which helps them. It also gives the shop a brand to recruit your next renter with. Some owners split it with renters, but it's your call."

**"I've been here 30 years, everyone knows me."**
> "Everyone who's been coming for 30 years does. The new families in town search on their phone first. This is how they find out what your regulars already know."

**"Too expensive / money's tight."** Use the CLOSER_SCRIPT handler. Anchor to value: "How many new regulars would it take to cover it? For most shops it's a handful of clients who come back all year."

**"Send me something" (with a client in the chair).**
> "Totally. Can I text you the demo? And when's your next gap, 3 or after close?" Only text with their verbal OK (see the texting rule in SETTER_SCRIPT).

**"My cousin does websites."** "Great. Take a look at the demo side by side. If theirs is better, go with them. We just want you to see what's possible for a shop like yours."

## 5. Setter openers and talking points

**Opener: barbershop (walk-in / old-school)**
> "Hey, is this the owner of [Shop]? [Name] from Vanguard. Quick one, I know you've probably got someone in the chair. I found [Shop] on Google, [X years / great reviews], but there's no website, just the listing. We built you a demo site with your services and hours already on it. Can I get 15 minutes with you on a slower day to show you?"

**Opener: salon / nail / lash (Instagram-heavy)**
> "Hi, is this [Name]? [Name] from Vanguard. I came across your work on Instagram, the [color/sets/lash] work is really good. I noticed the only way to book is the link in bio, so we built you a demo site that shows your menu, prices and gallery, with your Book button going right into your [Vagaro/Booksy]. Can I walk you through it for 15 minutes?"

**Opener: spa / massage**
> "Hi, [Name] from Vanguard. I found [Spa] on Google Maps, people clearly love it, but there's no website to see packages or buy a gift card. We built a demo showing what that could look like. Worth a 15-minute look before the holiday rush?"

**Talking points**
- "Keeps your booking app, doesn't replace it."
- "Answers the price/availability DMs for you."
- "Your work, your brand, not the app's."
- "Shows up when people Google '[niche] near me.'"
- Holiday gift cards (spa/massage/nails) from Oct onward.
- Booth-rent shops: "helps your renters and helps you fill chairs."
- Use one *verified* specific: their rating, years open, or a signature service.

**Don'ts:** Don't call it "cheap" or talk down their Instagram. Don't pitch during Saturday rush. Don't promise "more clients" as a number.

## 6. Demo guidance (`scripts/generate_demo.py`)

- **Preset:** `--industry beauty` for every sub-type (CTA default is "Book Your Session"). For old-school barbershops, override the tone with a plain `--tagline` (e.g. "Classic cuts on [Street] since [verified year]") so it doesn't read as a spa.
- **`--services`:** use their real menu from Booksy/Vagaro/Instagram highlights/Google (e.g. `"Haircut,Skin Fade,Beard Trim,Hot Towel Shave"`). Include prices only on the closer's call if they're verified.
- **`--booking_url`:** their existing booking-app link if public, otherwise leave the default.
- **`--highlights`:** verified only, copied from a source you checked:
  - `"4.8★:Google rating"` (and the review count if visible)
  - `"Since 1998:On Main St"` / `"25:Years in business"` from their listing, a news piece, or their own posts
  - `"Licensed:Master Barber"` only if on their profile or the state license lookup
  - Never make up client counts, "#1 in town," or testimonial names.
- **Sections to emphasize** (manual polish before the call, per DEMO_PLAYBOOK): hero with the Book button, the service menu, a gallery from IG photos (with permission language on the call: "we pulled these from your public page to show you"), team roster, hours + map + click-to-call, and policies.
- **CTA wording:** "Book Your Appointment," "Book with [Barber Name]," "See Prices & Book," "Buy a Gift Card" (spa), and for walk-in shops "Walk-ins Welcome · Call Now."

## 7. Timing, budgets, red flags

**Seasonality (rule of thumb)**
- Busy: prom/graduation (spring), wedding season (late spring–early fall), back-to-school (Aug), holidays (Nov–Dec). Owners have less time but feel the demand, which is good for a "be ready" pitch if booked ahead.
- Quieter: January–February and some summer weeks are good for getting attention and for "fill the slow weeks" framing.
- Gift-card angle: pitch spa/massage in Sep–Oct so the site is live before Black Friday.

**Budget / ability to pay**
- Solo artists and LMTs are the most price-sensitive. Foundation is the natural fit, and upfront payment may take a few days to gather. Don't invent payment plans outside `OFFER.md`, and flag it to the closer.
- Established salons, spas and multi-chair shops usually can pay upfront.
- Cash-heavy businesses may ask about paying by card or check. Confirm what Vanguard accepts before promising anything.

**Red flags (bad fit)**
- Home-based artist with no fixed location and no license visible (especially in services that require one).
- "Just rebuild my Instagram," or only wants a link-in-bio page for $50.
- Brand-new artist with no clients yet who expects the site to create a clientele from zero.
- A renter wanting a site for "the shop" without the owner's say.
- Constant rescheduling or no-shows to the closer call. That's how they'll run the project too.
- Businesses offering services that look outside licensing rules, or anything adult/"body rub" coded for massage. Walk away.

## 8. Upsells and Care Plan angles

- **Care Plan ($150–$250/mo):** menu and price updates, seasonal promos, new team member cards, holiday hours, gift-card promos, refreshing the gallery from their IG. "Text us the change, it's live, and you never log in."
- **Google Business Profile tune-up** (from the Local SEO item): categories, services, photos, booking link.
- **Gift card / e-gift setup** through their existing POS (Square etc.).
- **"Join the Team / Chair Available" page** for booth-rent recruiting.
- **Extra service pages** (bridal, color correction, men's grooming, couples massage) for local search. These justify Full Presence.
- **Review-request routine:** a QR card at the station linking to their Google review page.
