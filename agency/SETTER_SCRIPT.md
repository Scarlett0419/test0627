# Vanguard — Setter Cold Call Script

**Role:** Setter
**Goal:** Book a 15–30 minute consultation call between the prospect and the owner
**Hook:** "We already built you a demo — we just want to show you"

---

## Before You Dial

- Pull up their website (or confirm they don't have one)
- Know their first name and business name
- Have one specific thing to call out about their site (slow, no booking, looks outdated, mobile broken, no photos)
- Have the calendar link ready to send via text immediately after booking

---

## Opening — Cold Call Opener

**Goal:** Get 30 seconds of their attention by making it about them, not you.

> "Hey [First Name], this is [Your Name] — I'm reaching out from Vanguard. Quick question for you — are you the person who handles the website for [Business Name]?"

*(Wait for yes)*

> "Perfect. So I was actually looking at your site and noticed [specific gap — e.g., 'it looks like you don't have a way for people to book online' / 'the site's not loading well on mobile' / 'it looks like it hasn't been updated in a while']. We actually went ahead and built you a demo of what a redesigned version could look like — no strings attached, we just do this to show people what's possible. Would you be open to jumping on a quick 15-minute call so we can walk you through it?"

**Key:** Be specific about their gap. Generic openers die. The demo line creates curiosity and lowers resistance — they're not agreeing to buy anything, just to look.

---

## Objection Handlers

### "I'm not interested."

> "Totally fair — I wouldn't be either if someone cold called me out of nowhere. I'll just say this: we actually already built the demo for your business specifically — took us a few hours. All I'm asking for is 15 minutes to show it to you. If you hate it, no harm done. If you like it, we can talk. That's really it."

*(If still no:)*

> "Got it. Can I at least send you the link so you can take a look on your own time? No pressure."

---

### "We already have a website."

> "Yeah, I saw it — that's actually why I called. The site's there, but [specific issue]. What we built for you addresses that directly. A lot of our clients had websites too — they just weren't working. Takes 15 minutes to see if what we made is any better. Worth a look?"

---

### "Just send me an email."

> "I can do that. I'll be honest with you though — what I'd send you is a link to the demo, and it's way better to walk through it together so I can answer questions in real time. What does your schedule look like this week? Even just 15 minutes."

*(If they insist:)*

> "No problem. What's the best email? I'll send it over — and I'll include a link to get on my calendar if you want to talk after you've seen it."

---

### "It's not the right time / we're too busy right now."

> "I get that — this time of year [or: running a business] keeps you busy. Honestly, that's kind of why I wanted to reach out. A better website usually means more people finding you and booking on their own without you having to chase them. 15 minutes now might save you time later. When does it slow down a little for you — next week? Week after?"

---

### "How did you get my number?"

> "Your business is listed publicly — I was doing research in [city/niche] and came across [Business Name]. That's actually what led us to build the demo. Is this still a good number to use?"

---

### "Who are you again? What company?"

> "Vanguard — we do website design for local businesses. We've worked with [relevant social proof by niche — e.g., 'faith organizations like Christ United' / 'salons like Elixa Beauty' / 'gyms like MagniFit' / 'contractors like Vanguard Structures']. We're not a big agency — we're boutique, which means you'd actually be working directly with the owner."

---

## The Demo Hook — How to Drop It Naturally

Use this in your opener, but if you get a live conversation going before you drop it, work it in here:

> "Here's the thing — normally we'd just pitch you on our services, but we do something a little different. Before we ever reach out, we actually build a demo version of what your site could look like. So you're not just imagining it — you can literally see it. And we did that for [Business Name]. That's why I wanted to get on a call, just to show it to you."

**Rule:** Always frame the demo as something you already built for them — not something you'll build if they're interested. It's already done. That's the hook.

---

## Booking Close — Exact Words

Once they've expressed any openness:

> "Great — let me grab a time that works for you. I'm going to text you a link right now to get on the calendar. It's a 15–30 minute call, and you'll be talking directly with the owner — not a salesperson. Does [day] or [day] work better for you?"

*(Get a day, then narrow to a time)*

> "Perfect. I'll send the link right now — just click it and grab the time. You should get a confirmation text and email. Does that work?"

**Then immediately text them the calendar link.** Don't wait. Don't email. Text.

---

## Voicemail Script

Keep it under 25 seconds. Sound calm, not salesy.

> "Hey [First Name], this is [Your Name] from Vanguard. We actually built a demo redesign of your website for [Business Name] and wanted to walk you through it — takes about 15 minutes. Give me a call back at [number] or I'll try you again in a couple days. Talk soon."

**Do not** leave your whole pitch. Create curiosity. Let the demo line do the work.

---

---

## Setter Rules

1. **Never oversell.** Your only job is to get the call booked.
2. **Always reference something specific** about their website or business.
3. **Drop the demo hook early** — it's your biggest differentiator.
4. **Text the calendar link immediately** after they verbally agree.
5. **Don't explain pricing.** If they ask, say "that's a great question for the call — it depends on what you actually need."
6. **Don't badmouth their current site.** Be curious and helpful, not critical.
7. **Log every touchpoint in CRM the same day.**
