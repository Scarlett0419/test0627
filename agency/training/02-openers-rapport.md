# Setter Training 02: Opening the Cold Call and Building Rapport

**Who this is for:** Vanguard setters calling US local service businesses (salons, barbershops, gyms, roofers, contractors, auto repair, towing, pet groomers, cleaners, restaurants, daycares) that have no website or a weak one.
**What it covers:** the first 10 seconds, word-for-word openers, rapport with busy owners, getting from "hello" to a booked call, and how to practice.
**Use with:** `agency/SETTER_SCRIPT.md` (the master script), `agency/OFFER_ONE_PAGER.md` (what you can and can't promise), and the playbook for your niche in `agency/industries/`.

> **Your only job on this call:** get the owner to agree to a 15–30 minute call with our owner to see the demo we already built for them. You're not selling the website. You're selling the conversation. (OFFER_ONE_PAGER)

---

## Hard rules (read these first)

1. **No fake familiarity.** Don't say "Hey buddy, how've you been?" to a stranger. Don't use a tone that suggests you've met. Never say "so-and-so told me to call", "we spoke last month" or "returning your call" unless it's true.
2. **Never claim a referral, a relationship or a past conversation that doesn't exist.**
3. **Say who you are and what company you're with in your first sentence.** Never pose as a customer, a supplier or "Google."
4. **Only say true, checked things about their business.** "Great reviews" only if you saw them. "Open since 2009" only if a listing says so and other sources don't disagree. If you're not sure, leave it out. (The industry playbooks say the same.)
5. **The demo has to exist before you dial.** "We already built it" has to be true.
6. **Follow the compliance rules in SETTER_SCRIPT.md:** scrub every number against the DNC list, call only between 8am and 9pm in *their* time zone, and text only after a clear verbal "yes." Anyone who says "don't call me" goes on the internal do-not-call list the same day.
7. **No promised results** ("you'll rank #1", "you'll get 20 more clients"), and **no made-up prices**. If they ask the price, follow Module 03: honest answer, approved ballpark on the second ask.

These rules aren't only about ethics. Owners of small businesses talk to each other. One setter who lies about a referral costs us a whole town.

## 1. The first 10 seconds

When a stranger picks up, they're deciding one thing: *"Is this a telemarketer I can hang up on?"* Your opener needs to answer that question **honestly**, in a way that makes them curious enough to give you 30 more seconds.

The 30 Minutes to President's Club hosts (Armand Farrokh and Nick Cegelski) put the goal plainly: the first 60 seconds exist to earn the next 60 seconds ([summary of *Cold Calling Sucks (And That's Why It Works)*](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-armand-farrokh-and-nick-cegelski/)). You aren't trying to book in 10 seconds. You're trying not to get hung up on.

### Tone: sound like a calm person, not a salesperson

- **Calm, steady and a little slower than feels natural.** Nerves make you fast. Fast sounds like a pitch. The 30MPC book advises against a rushed, upward-inflected or robotic delivery because it makes an opener sound canned ([source](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-armand-farrokh-and-nick-cegelski/)).
- **Make statements go down at the end.** "This is Jordan with Vanguard." should sound like a full stop, not "This is Jordan with Vanguard?" Upward inflection on a statement sounds unsure and asks for approval.
- **Keep it friendly and low pressure.** Alex Hormozi teaches that non-verbal delivery (emphasis, tone up or down, pace, volume) matters more than word choice, and that "a prepared script that sounds like a robot loses." Slow and low signals that what you're saying is important and can be trusted (from the `hormozi-sales` skill in this repo, which is built from his public sales training videos).
- **Smile before you dial.** People can hear it. Stand up if you can. Hormozi's tip is to reset your mood between calls (his own trick is a mini trampoline), because mood leaks into tone.
- **Respect their time out loud.** Kevin "KD" Dorsey's version: every prospect wants to know who you are and why you're calling, and if you respect their time now they'll respect your pitch later ([compiled KD lessons](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-kevin-dorsey/)).

### Pace: say less

- Your opener (name, company, honest framing, permission ask) should take **under 10 seconds**.
- Then **stop talking** and let them answer. Silence after a question is your friend. Jeb Blount's telephone framework ends with: ask for what you want, then stop talking ([*Fanatical Prospecting* summary](https://www.supersummary.com/fanatical-prospecting/summary/)).

### Pattern interrupts: be different from the last 10 callers

Local owners get robocalls, "Google listing verification" scams and SEO spam every day. Their hang-up reflex is trained on a pattern: fake cheer, "How are you doing today?", then a pitch. A **pattern interrupt** breaks that pattern so their brain pays attention instead of running on autopilot.

The pattern interrupts that work for us are all **honest**:

| Interrupt | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Admitting it's a cold call** | "I'll be honest, this is a cold call." | No telemarketer says this. It lowers their guard because you just said the thing they were thinking. 30MPC calls this "disarming the prospect with brutal honesty" ([Gong / Armand Farrokh](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls)). |
| **"Already built it"** | "We already built you a demo." | Nobody else calling them has done work up front. It's our core hook (SETTER_SCRIPT). |
| **Naming their likely objection first** | "You probably get a lot of calls from website people." | Chris Voss calls this an *accusation audit*: you say the negative thing they're thinking before they can, which defuses it ([Shortform summary of *Never Split the Difference*](https://www.shortform.com/blog/chris-voss-accusation-audit/)). |
| **Asking for a little help** | "I'm hoping you can help me out for a second." | People like to help. KD Dorsey and Josh Braun both use versions of this ([KD lessons](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-kevin-dorsey/), [Braun method summary](https://www.11x.ai/guides/josh-braun-cold-outreach-method)). |

**The line you never cross:** a pattern interrupt that relies on confusing them about who you are ("Hey John, it's Mike!" with a tone that says "you know me") is deception. Don't do it. Hormozi's "John?" opener with a rising tone, as if you already know him, is written for **inbound leads who opted in**. It is not for cold calls.

### Permission-based openers vs. "How are you?"

Gong (a call-recording company) has published data on cold-call openers. It's vendor data and "success" means a booked meeting, so treat it as directional rather than as law:

- **Permission-based opener** (context, own the cold call, ask permission): **11.18%** success in Gong's 300M-call analysis written by Armand Farrokh ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls)).
- **"How's your day going?":** **7.6%** in the same analysis. Gong warns it sounds like telemarketing and signals that you don't really care ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls)).
- **"Did I catch you at a bad time?":** the lowest of the four tested, at **2.15%** ([Gong, 300M calls](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls)). An earlier Gong study of 90,380 calls found it **40% less likely** to book a meeting than the baseline (0.9% vs 1.5%) ([Gong, 2018](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines)).
- **Stating a clear reason for the call:** **2.1x** higher success ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines), [Gong cold call stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats)).

**Watch out for this one:** Gong's top opener in the 2018 study was **"How have you been?"** (10.01%) ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines)). **We don't use it.** Asking a stranger "how have you been" implies you've talked before. That's fake familiarity, and it breaks Hard Rule 1.

**What this means for you:**
- **Default opener:** the honest, tailored permission opener (Opener A below).
- **"How are you?"** isn't banned, but it buys nothing. If you use it, mean it, and keep going straight into your reason.
- **"Did I catch you at a bad time?"** only works with humor and confidence, and you must never back off (Opener B shows how). Gong's specific warning is that backtracking makes you "seem like a pest."
- **Always give a reason.** Blount teaches a "because" statement: people want a reason before they give you their time ([Shortform on *Fanatical Prospecting*](https://www.shortform.com/blog/prospecting-channels/)). Ours is the strongest reason there is: *we already built something for you.*

## 2. Word-for-word openers (tailored to our demo hook)

Fill the brackets from your pre-call research. **Every bracket must be true.** If you can't fill a "specific thing" bracket honestly, use "no website, just the Google listing," which is specific and true for most of our leads.

### Opener A: honest cold call (default)

> "Hi, is this [First Name]?"
> *(Yes)*
> "[First Name], this is [Your Name] with Vanguard. I'll be honest, this is a cold call. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called, and then you can tell me if it's worth keeping going?"
> *(Most say "go ahead" or "make it quick")*
> "Appreciate it. I was looking up [barbershops] in [city] and came across [Business] on Google. [You've got 180-plus reviews / people keep mentioning your fades]. But there's no actual website, just the Google listing. So we went ahead and built you a demo of one, with your services and hours already on it. It's done. I'd love to show it to you. It's a 15-minute call with our owner. Would you be open to that?"

**Why it works:** It's honest, it gives them control ("you tell me"), it's specific, and the demo creates curiosity with no commitment.

### Opener B: "Did I catch you at a bad time?" (with humor, no backing off)

> "Hi [First Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. Did I catch you at a bad time?"
> *(If "no, what's up?")*
> "Perfect, I'll be quick. I'm calling because we built a demo website for [Business]…" *(go to the reason line)*
> *(If "kind of, what is it?")*
> "Fair enough. 20 seconds, then you can hang up on me. We already built a demo website for [Business]. I just want to get 15 minutes on your calendar to show it to you."
> *(If "yes, I'm with a client")*
> "No problem at all. When's your next break, 20 minutes from now or after 5?" *(Write it down. Call back exactly then.)*

**Why it works (and when it doesn't):** It shows respect, but the data says it's a weak opener when you say it timidly. The fix is in how you handle what comes next: keep moving or lock in a specific callback time. Never say "Oh sorry, I'll call back another time" and hang up.

### Opener C: reason first, with their Google reviews

> "Hi, is this [First Name], the owner of [Business]?"
> *(Yes)*
> "[First Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. The reason I'm calling: I was reading your Google reviews. [Rating] stars, and people keep mentioning [real theme: how good you are with nervous dogs / the same-day repairs]. But when someone searches you, there's no website to land on. So we built one. It's a demo with your services on it, already finished. Can I grab 15 minutes this week to show you?"

**Why it works:** "The reason I'm calling" is the Gong 2.1x behavior. Mentioning something real from their reviews shows you did homework, and that's rapport you earned honestly.

### Opener D: accusation audit (for owners who sound guarded)

> "Hi [First Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. You probably get a lot of calls from website people, so I'll keep this short and different. We didn't call to pitch you. We already built a demo site for [Business], using your real services and info. All I'd like is 15 minutes to show it to you. If you hate it, no harm done. Fair?"

**Why it works:** Naming their suspicion first takes the sting out of it (Voss). "Fair?" invites a yes without pressure.

### Opener E: "help me out"

> "Hi [First Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. I'm hoping you can help me out for a second. We built a demo website for [Business], and I'm trying to figure out who I should show it to. Is that you, or someone else?"

**Why it works:** It's a soft question they can answer easily ("that's me"), and it qualifies the decision-maker at the same time. It's honest because the demo exists and you do need the right person.

### Opener F: "I know you're busy" (trades, towing, anyone on a job)

> "Hi [First Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. I know I'm probably catching you on a job, so I'll be quick. We built a free demo website for [Business]. Can I grab 15 minutes with you, early morning or after you're off site, to show it to you?"

**Why it works:** You named their reality, offered two specific windows, and asked for nothing right now.

### Opener G: they already have a weak site (match SETTER_SCRIPT, don't trash it)

> "Hi [First Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. Quick one: are you the person who handles the website for [Business]?"
> *(Yes)*
> "I was on your site earlier and noticed [there's no way to book online / it's hard to use on a phone / the hours aren't on it]. We went ahead and built a demo of what a refreshed version could look like. No strings attached. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call so we can walk you through it?"

**Why it works:** You pointed at one concrete gap, framed it neutrally, and offered something already made. Never call their site "bad" or "outdated." (SETTER_SCRIPT Rule 6)

### Opener H: gatekeeper (front desk, spouse, service writer)

> "Hi, this is [Your Name] with Vanguard. I'm trying to reach the owner. Is that [Owner Name]?"
> *(Who's calling / what's it about?)*
> "Totally fair to ask. We built a demo website for the shop, and I wanted to show it to [him/her]. When's the best time to catch [Owner Name] for two minutes?"

**Why it works:** Honest, short, and it treats the gatekeeper as someone who can help, not an obstacle. In trades the spouse or office manager often influences the decision too (`industries/trades.md`). Be warm.

## 3. Rapport with busy local owners

Rapport on a cold call isn't small talk about the weather. For a busy owner, rapport means: *"This person respects my time, knows something real about my business, and talks like a normal human."*

### Use their name, but naturally
- Say it at the start and maybe once more before the booking ask. Don't say it every sentence. That's a scripted-sales tell.
- Use the title they use publicly: "Coach," "Doc," "Chef." (`industries/fitness.md` says the same for Coach/Sensei/Professor.)

### Mention one specific, true thing
Pick **one** from your research and say it plainly:
- Reviews: "People keep mentioning how fast you get them in."
- Years open: "Looks like you've been in [city] a long time." (only if verified)
- Services: "I saw you do [ceramic coating / emergency tarping / kids' BJJ]."
- Their work: "Your before-and-afters on Facebook are really good."

One specific thing beats three generic compliments. Generic flattery ("Love your business!") sounds like a script. Specific observations sound like a person who looked.

### Mirroring (Chris Voss)
Repeat their last 1–3 key words as a gentle question, then **go quiet**. The Black Swan Group describes it as repeating the last few words with a downward-inflecting voice and then going silent ([Black Swan Group](https://www.blackswanltd.com/newsletter/the-black-swan-what-it-is-and-how-to-find-it)).

> Owner: "Honestly, most of our people come from Facebook."
> You: "Mostly Facebook?" *(pause)*
> Owner: "Yeah, and Google I guess, people find the listing and call…"

They'll keep talking, and you'll learn what you need to know without interrogating them.

### Labeling (Chris Voss)
A label names what they seem to be feeling, starting with "It sounds like…", "It seems like…" or "It looks like…" ([Shortform summary of *Never Split the Difference*](https://www.shortform.com/blog/never-split-the-difference-takeaways/)). Say it, then pause.

- "Sounds like you're slammed right now."
- "Seems like word of mouth has been really good to you."
- "Sounds like you've been burned by a website person before."
- "It looks like you've built this whole thing off referrals."

Labels make people feel heard, and people who feel heard stay on the phone. If you got it right, they'll often say "Yeah, exactly" and explain more.

### Match their energy (within reason)
- **Fast, clipped owner** (roofer on a ladder, tow dispatcher): be shorter and get to the point. "Two things, then I'm out of your hair."
- **Chatty owner** (salon owner on a slow Tuesday): slow down, laugh with them, let them talk.
- **Stressed or annoyed owner:** go calmer and lower, not louder. Voss's "late-night FM DJ voice" (slow, low, calm) is meant to bring the temperature down ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/never-split-the-difference-takeaways/)).
- Don't match rudeness or swearing. Stay friendly.

### Humor (light and self-aware)
A small laugh lowers everyone's guard. Keep it about the situation or about yourself, never about them.
- "I know, the last thing you wanted today was a website guy calling."
- "20 seconds, then you're free to hang up on me."
- Hormozi uses humor to say hard things without making people defensive. Keep it light and real.

### Sound like a person, not a script
- **Know the script well enough to put it in your own words.** Read it 50 times, then stop reading it on calls.
- **Use contractions and normal words:** "we built you" not "we have created for your organization."
- **Allow small, natural pauses.** A perfect, unbroken paragraph sounds read aloud.
- **React to what they actually said.** If they say "We're closed Mondays," don't barrel on. Say "Oh, so I caught you on your day off? I'll be quick."

### Talk balance on a cold call
Here's something surprising: Gong found that on *successful* cold calls the rep talks **about 55% of the time** ([Gong cold call stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats)). A cold call isn't a discovery call. The owner didn't ask for this conversation, so **you** carry it: clear reason, short story, easy questions. Don't fire question after question at them. Ask 2–3 light questions (Section 4) and save the deep discovery for our owner's call.

## 4. From opener to interest to booking

Here's the full shape of a good call. It usually runs 2–5 minutes.

```
OPENER (10s) → REASON + DEMO HOOK (15s) → 2–3 LIGHT QUESTIONS (60–90s)
   → DEMO OFFER (15s) → BOOKING ASK WITH TWO OPTIONS → TEXT/EMAIL CONSENT → SEND LINK NOW
```

### Step 1: Light questions (pick 2–3, don't interrogate)

These help the owner notice the gap for themselves. Ask, then listen, mirror and label.

1. **"How do most new customers find you right now?"**
   *Listen for:* word of mouth, Google, Facebook/Instagram, Angi/Thumbtack, walk-ins.
2. **"When someone hears about you from a friend and Googles [Business], what do they find?"**
   *Usually:* "The Google listing, I guess." Label it: "So the referral does the work, and then there's nowhere to send them."
3. **"Do you get a lot of calls or DMs asking about hours, prices or whether you do [X]?"**
   *Usually:* "All day." Label it: "Sounds like you're answering the same questions over and over."
4. *(Trades/auto/towing)* **"When you're on a job and the phone rings, what happens to that call?"**
5. *(Salon/gym)* **"Right now, how do people book: the app link, DMs or calling?"**

You don't need all of them. If they light up on question 1, go straight to the demo offer.

### Step 2: The demo offer (tie it to what they just said)

> "That's actually why I called. We already built a demo site for [Business]. It's got your [services / menu / class schedule] and a [Book Now / Get a Free Quote / Call Now] button right at the top, so people who look you up have somewhere to go. It's not a template. It's built around your actual business. I'd like to walk you through it. It takes 15 minutes, with our owner, the person who'd actually build it. No obligation."

Things you **can** say (OFFER_ONE_PAGER): you'll talk directly with the owner who builds it, the call is free and has no obligation, and they'll see the demo.
Things you **can't** say: an exact price for their business (Module 03 covers the approved ballpark), "you'll get X more customers," "you'll rank #1," or "live in 14 days from today."

### Step 3: The booking ask, with two time options

Ask for the meeting directly, then **stop talking**.

> "Does Tuesday or Wednesday work better for you?"
> *(They pick one.)*
> "Morning or afternoon?"
> *(They pick one.)*
> "Great. Is this a cell I can text the calendar link and the demo to?"
> *(Wait for a clear yes. Log "SMS consent: yes, [date/time], verbal" in the CRM. If they don't say yes, ask for their email instead.)*
> "Sending it now. Just tap it and grab the [Tuesday morning] slot. You'll get a confirmation by text and email. Sound good?"

**Send the Calendly link immediately, while they're still on the phone if you can.** Then: "Did that come through?" That one question saves a lot of no-shows.

**Why two options:** "Does Tuesday or Wednesday work better?" is easier to answer than "When are you free?", which makes them stop and think about their whole week. It keeps you in control without pressure, and they can always say "Neither, how about Friday?"

**Respect the niche's schedule** (from the industry playbooks): salons and barbers on a slower weekday, gyms between class blocks (roughly 10am–2pm), trades early morning or after they're off site, restaurants 2–4pm and never during service, auto at opening or in the mid-afternoon lull.

### If they say "just send me something"
Don't argue. Use the SETTER_SCRIPT line: "I can do that. I'll be honest though, what I'd send is the demo link, and it's a lot better to walk through it together so you can ask questions. What does your week look like? Even 15 minutes." If they insist, get their email and send the demo plus the calendar link.

## 5. Niche-flavored openers

These build on the openers in each `industries/*.md` file. The same rule applies: every specific detail must be checked.

### Salon / barbershop
> "Hi, is this [Name], the owner of [Shop]? [Your Name] with Vanguard. Quick one, I know you've probably got someone in the chair. I found [Shop] on Google, [verified: 4.9 stars / people rave about the fades], but there's no website, just the listing [and the Booksy link]. We built you a demo site with your services and hours already on it, and the Book button goes straight to your [Booksy/Vagaro], so nothing changes on your end. Can I grab 15 minutes on a slower day to show you?"

*Rapport angle:* compliment the **work** (cuts, color, nail sets), not "the business." Talking point: "Keeps your booking app, doesn't replace it." Don't call Saturdays or Friday afternoons.

### Trades (roofing, plumbing, HVAC, fencing, painting, landscaping)
> "Hi [Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. I know you're probably heading to a job, so I'll be quick. I found [Business] on Google, solid reviews for [roofing] in [city], but there's no website, just the map listing. We went ahead and built you a demo with your services and a 'Get a Free Quote' button on it. Can I grab 15 minutes early morning or after you're off site to walk you through it?"

*Rapport angle:* respect the job site. Label: "Sounds like you're booked out." Ask: "When you're up on a roof and the phone rings, what happens to that call?" Don't promise rankings and never call them "unlicensed."

### Gym / CrossFit / martial arts
> "Hey, is this [Name], the owner of [Gym]? [Your Name] with Vanguard. I'll be quick, I know you're probably between classes. I found [Gym] on Google, [verified: great reviews / open since 20XX], but there's no website, just the Facebook page. We built you a demo site with your programs and a 'Book a Free Class' button already on it. Can I get 15 minutes to walk you through it?"

*Rapport angle:* use "Coach" if that's what they go by. From September to November: "so it's live before the January rush." Don't call during 5–8am or 4–8pm class blocks.

### Auto repair / towing / detailing
> **Repair:** "Hi [Name], [Your Name] with Vanguard. I came across [Shop] on Google, [verified: people keep saying you're honest on pricing], and noticed there's no website, just the listings. We already built you a demo with your services, hours and a click-to-call button. Can the owner spare 15 minutes this week to see it?"
>
> **Towing:** "I'll be quick in case a call comes in. We built [Business] a demo site with a big 'Call Now' button and your service area, so people who need a tow can find and call you fast. Want me to grab 15 minutes later today or tomorrow to show you?"

*Rapport angle:* "Off the lift" timing. Ask for the owner by name and be polite to the service writer. Only mention 24/7 if they really run 24/7. Don't claim certifications.

### Restaurant / café / food truck
> "Hi, is this [Name]? [Your Name] with Vanguard. I timed this for between lunch and dinner, so hopefully I'm not catching you mid-rush. I found [Restaurant] on Google, [verified: people keep mentioning the birria], but there's no website, so people can't see the menu or hours anywhere except the listing. We built you a demo with your menu, hours and a catering request form. When's a quiet 15 minutes, before service tomorrow?"

*Rapport angle:* call 2–4pm only. Mention a **dish** people rave about in reviews. Be patient and speak clearly if English is their second language (`industries/pet-and-local-services.md`). Don't promise savings on delivery-app fees.

## 6. Mistakes that kill calls

| Mistake | What it sounds like | Why it kills the call | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pitching too early** | "We do custom websites, SEO, mobile responsive, booking integration…" in the first 15 seconds | They haven't agreed to listen yet. A feature list is noise. | Earn 30 seconds first (permission), then give **one** reason: the demo. |
| **Sounding scripted** | Perfect, fast, sing-song delivery with the name in every sentence | Triggers the "telemarketer" reflex instantly | Learn the script, then say it in your own words. Slow down. Pitch goes down at the end. |
| **Talking over them** | Owner starts "Look, I'm not—" and you keep going | Tells them you don't care what they think. Rapport is gone. | Stop the moment they speak. Mirror or label what they said. |
| **"Is now a good time?" done badly** | "Is now a good time?…Oh, okay, sorry, I'll call back!" *click* | Gives them an easy exit, and backtracking makes you sound like a pest (Gong) | Use Opener A or B. If it's truly a bad time, book a **specific** callback: "20 minutes or after 5?" |
| **"How are you today?" + pause** | Fake-cheery small talk with a stranger | Gong data puts it below permission openers, and it sounds like telemarketing | Get to who you are and why you're calling. |
| **Overselling** | "This will double your customers!" "You'll be #1 on Google!" | Not allowed (OFFER_ONE_PAGER), not believable, and the closer has to walk it back | Sell the 15-minute look, not the website. |
| **Leading with price, or dodging it** | Volunteering "it's about two grand" before they ask, or saying "that's for the owner" twice when they do | Leading with price kills curiosity; dodging twice kills trust | Don't volunteer it. If they ask, use Module 03: "It depends on what you need" + one fit question, then the ballpark on the second ask. |
| **Badmouthing their current site or setup** | "Your site is really outdated" / "Facebook isn't a real website" | They built it, or their nephew did. They'll get defensive. | Be neutral and curious: "I noticed there's no way to book online." |
| **Faking a detail** | "Love your 5-star reviews!" (they have 3.8) | They know their own rating, so you lose all trust | One verified, specific thing, or "no website, just the listing." |
| **Interrogating** | Seven discovery questions in a row | It's a cold call. They didn't sign up for an interview. | Ask 2–3 light questions, then the demo offer. |
| **Weak or no ask** | "So… let me know if you're interested?" | No ask means no booking | "Does Tuesday or Wednesday work better?" Then stop talking. |
| **Not sending the link right away** | "I'll shoot that over later today" | Interest fades within minutes | Send it while they're on the phone and confirm it came through. |

## 7. "Who is this?" / "How did you get my number?"

Answer **honestly, calmly and briefly**, then go back to the reason for the call. Don't get defensive. It's a fair question, and owners get a lot of scam calls.

**"Who is this?"**
> "It's [Your Name] with Vanguard. We build websites for local businesses like yours. I'm calling because we already built a demo site for [Business] and I'd like to show it to you."

**"Who are you again? What company?"**
> "Vanguard. We do website design for local businesses. We're a small, boutique team, so you'd be working directly with the owner, not passed around."
> *(Only name past clients that SETTER_SCRIPT lists as approved social proof for that niche. Never make one up.)*

**"How did you get my number?"**
> "Totally fair question. Your business number is listed publicly on [Google / your Facebook page / Yelp]. I was looking up [niche] in [city] and came across [Business], and that's what led us to build the demo. Is this still the best number for the business?"

**"Is this a scam? / Are you with Google?"**
> "No, and I'm glad you asked, because there's a lot of that going around. We're not Google and we're not calling about your listing. I'm [Your Name] with Vanguard, a website company. We built a demo site for you, and I'm happy to send the link so you can look at it before you agree to anything."

**If it was a personal cell that happened to be listed:**
> "Sorry about that. It was the number on your [Google listing]. Is there a better business number, or would you rather I not call this one?"
> *(If they say don't call: apologize, end the call, and add them to the do-not-call list today.)*

**Never** say "a friend of yours gave it to me," "you signed up on our site," "you requested info," or "I'm returning your call" unless it's true.

## 8. Practice drills

Reading this once won't make you good. Reps will. Plan on **30 minutes a day for your first two weeks**, then 3 sessions a week.

### Drill 1: Opener out loud (daily, 5 min)
- Say Openers A, C and D out loud **10 times each**, standing up, into your phone's voice recorder.
- Play one back. Check: Did statements go down at the end? Did you take under 10 seconds to reach the permission ask? Did it sound like you, or like you were reading?

### Drill 2: The "first 10 seconds" gauntlet (pairs, 10 min)
- Partner plays the owner and answers **only** with one of these: hang-up threat ("I'm busy"), "Who is this?", "How'd you get my number?", silence, "Go ahead."
- You deliver the opener and handle what comes back. Swap after 10 rounds.
- Goal: no freezing, no backtracking, and an honest answer every time.

### Drill 3: Niche roleplay (pairs, 15 min)
Partner picks a card and plays that owner with real personality:
- Barber with a client in the chair, short and distracted
- Roofer driving to a job, loud background, wants it fast
- Gym owner who's friendly but "burned by a web guy before"
- Restaurant owner at 3:30pm, a little suspicious
- Tow dispatcher who might cut you off for a call

Run the **full call**: opener, 2–3 questions, demo offer, two-option booking ask, text consent. The partner scores you with the scorecard below.

### Drill 4: Mirror and label reps (pairs, 5 min)
- Partner says one-liners: "We get everything from Facebook." "I've been doing this 22 years." "Websites are a waste of money." "I'm swamped."
- You respond with a **mirror** or a **label** only, no pitching. Example: "Sounds like you've been burned before."

### Drill 5: Call review (weekly, 30 min)
- If Vanguard records calls, pick **3 of your calls**: one booked, one hung up, and one where you got a "send me info."
- **Only record or review live calls the way Vanguard has approved.** Some US states require everyone on the call to consent to recording. Ask your manager before you record anything.
- Listen with the scorecard. Find the **exact second** each call turned, good or bad.
- Hormozi's advice: when you're "hot" (several bookings in a row), study those calls hardest. Copy what you did right.

### Scorecard (use for roleplays and call review)

| # | Check | Yes / No |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Said name + Vanguard in the first sentence | |
| 2 | Opener under ~10 seconds, calm pace, statements going down | |
| 3 | Gave a clear reason ("I'm calling because… we already built…") | |
| 4 | Mentioned one specific, **true** thing about their business | |
| 5 | No fake familiarity, no false claims, no price | |
| 6 | Asked 2–3 light questions (no interrogation) | |
| 7 | Used at least one mirror or label | |
| 8 | Never talked over them | |
| 9 | Clear booking ask with two time options, then stopped talking | |
| 10 | Got text consent (or email) and sent the link during the call | |

**Target:** 8/10 in roleplay before you go live, and 9/10 on reviewed calls by the end of week 2.

## 9. One-page cheat sheet

```
VANGUARD SETTER — OPEN & BOND CHEAT SHEET
Goal: book a 15–30 min call with our owner to see the demo. Sell the call, not the site.

BEFORE YOU DIAL
□ DNC-scrubbed, 8am–9pm THEIR time   □ Demo built + tested on phone
□ Owner's first name + business name  □ ONE true, specific detail (reviews/years/service)
□ Right time for the niche (salon weekday lull / gym 10–2 / trades early or after site /
  restaurant 2–4 / auto at opening or mid-afternoon)

FIRST 10 SECONDS — calm, slower, statements go DOWN, smile
"Hi, is this [Name]? … [Name], this is [Me] with Vanguard. I'll be honest, this is a
 cold call. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I called, and you tell me if it's
 worth keeping going?"

REASON + HOOK
"I was looking up [niche] in [city] and found [Business] on Google — [true detail].
 But there's no website, just the listing. So we went ahead and built you a demo —
 your services, your hours. It's already done."

2–3 LIGHT QUESTIONS (then listen, mirror, label)
• "How do most new customers find you right now?"
• "When someone hears about you and Googles you, what do they find?"
• "Do you get a lot of calls asking hours/prices/do you do X?"
Mirror: repeat their last 1–3 words + pause.   Label: "Sounds like you're slammed."

DEMO OFFER
"That's why I called — I'd love to walk you through it. 15 minutes, with our owner,
 the person who'd build it. No obligation."

BOOK IT
"Does Tuesday or Wednesday work better?" → "Morning or afternoon?" → STOP TALKING
"Is this a cell I can text the calendar link and demo to?" (clear YES → log consent;
 no → email). Send it NOW. "Did that come through?"

QUICK SAVES
Who is this?        → "[Me] with Vanguard — we built a demo site for [Business]."
How'd you get #?    → "It's listed publicly on [Google]. I was looking up [niche] in [city]."
Bad time?           → "No problem — 20 minutes or after 5?" (then call back EXACTLY then)
Just send info      → "Happy to — it's honestly better to walk through together. 15 min?"
Price?              → "Depends on what you need." 2nd ask: "Most builds land around two thousand, one-time. Demo's free." (Module 03)
Don't call me       → Apologize, end call, add to do-not-call list TODAY.

NEVER
✗ Fake familiarity ("how've you been?")  ✗ Fake referral / "returning your call"
✗ Unverified compliments or facts         ✗ Made-up prices, rankings, "X more customers"
✗ Talking over them                       ✗ Trashing their site
✗ Backing off "is now a good time?"       ✗ Text without a clear verbal yes
```
