# Vanguard — Hiring & Onboarding Setters

How to go from a pile of applications to a setter who is dialing, honestly and quickly. Covers screening, the interview (what to say, what to ask, what to listen for), the role-play, the scorecard, the offer, the paperwork, and the first two weeks.

**Related docs:** pay and commission rules → `SETTER_PAY_PLAN.md` · week-1 training and certification → `DISCORD_ONBOARDING.md` §3 (Slack works the same way) · the script they'll learn → `SETTING_CALL_SCRIPT.html` / `Vanguard-Setting-Call-Script.pdf` · training modules → `training/`.

---

## 0. Before you interview anyone

**Don't start anyone dialing until the phones work.** Commission-only setters who sit for two weeks with no numbers, no leads or no way for the closer to take payment will quit. You can still interview now; set the start date for when these are live:

- [ ] GHL phone numbers bought and A2P / calling setup working
- [ ] DNC scrubbing in place for the first lead list
- [ ] Contract template + Stripe payment link ready (so a "yes" on your closing call becomes money)
- [ ] Calendly connected to GHL, Slack notifications working
- [ ] At least 300 scrubbed leads with demos built (about 2 days of dialing per setter)

**How many to hire:** start with **1–2 setters**, not 3+. At target, one setter books about 15 calls a week and sends you 13–14 builds a month, and you can deliver about 5–6. Add setters when delivery can keep up.

## 1. The hiring funnel

| Stage | Time per candidate | Who passes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Application screen | 2 min | Meets the basics (below) |
| 2. Voice-note screen | 3 min to review | Clear, confident, followed the instructions |
| 3. Live interview + role-play | 25 min | Scores 18+/25 on the scorecard with no 1s, and coachability 4+ |
| 4. Offer + paperwork | 15 min | Signs the agreement and compliance acknowledgment |
| 5. Week 1 training → certification | 5 days | Passes the certification checklist |
| 6. Day-14 review | 15 min | Hits the ramp targets (§7) |

Expect roughly 1 good setter from every 10–15 applicants. That's normal for commission-only roles.

---

## 2. Stage 1 — Application screen (2 minutes each)

**Pass if all are true:**
- Can work **US business hours**: at least 4 hours a day between 9am and 6pm in the leads' time zone (for Eastern Time that's 2pm–11pm UK, and Bangladesh evening/night).
- Fluent, easy-to-understand English.
- Has a laptop, a headset with a mic, stable internet, and a quiet place to call from.
- Followed any instruction in your job post. **Tip:** add a line to the post such as "Start your application with the word *Vanguard*". It filters out mass-appliers instantly.

**Nice to have, not required:** cold-calling, appointment-setting, customer-service or sales experience. Coachability beats experience.

**Reject quietly:** no hours overlap, copy-pasted application, didn't follow the instruction.

---

## 3. Stage 2 — Voice-note screen

Send this to everyone who passes Stage 1. It cuts most no-shows and shows you their phone voice before you spend 25 minutes on a call.

> **Message: invite to the voice screen**
>
> Hi [Name], thanks for applying to the appointment setter role at Vanguard.
>
> Next step is quick: send me a voice note or Loom (60–90 seconds) where you:
> 1. Introduce yourself in 2–3 sentences: where you're based, and any calling or sales experience.
> 2. Read this opener out loud as if you were on a real call:
>
> *"Hi, is this Mike? Mike, it's [your name] from Vanguard. I'm looking at Mike's Auto Repair on Google right now: 4.7 stars from 89 reviews, great work. I noticed there's no website though, is that right? So believe it or not, we actually went ahead and built you one, a demo using your real services. Is that something you'd even want to see?"*
>
> Please send it by [day]. If it's a fit, I'll send you a link to book a 25-minute interview.
>
> [Your name], Vanguard

**What to listen for:**

| Good sign | Bad sign |
|---|---|
| Sounds like a person talking, not reading | Monotone, rushed, mumbled |
| Slows down, pauses after the question | Rushes through the question without a beat |
| Warm and calm | Pushy or nervous to the point of being hard to follow |
| Sent on time, followed all the steps | Late, missing part 1 or 2, or sent text instead |

Pass the best 3–5 to the interview.

> **Message: invite to the interview**
>
> Hi [Name], thanks for the voice note, that was good. Let's do a 25-minute video interview. Book a time here: [Calendly link].
>
> Before the call, please read the setting call script (attached) once. We'll do a short role-play together, and I'm more interested in how you take feedback than in a perfect first try.
>
> [Your name], Vanguard

Attach `Vanguard-Setting-Call-Script.pdf`.

## 4. Stage 3 — The interview (25 minutes)

Run it the same way every time so you can compare candidates fairly. Camera on, both of you.

### 0:00 – 2:00 · Open and set the agenda

> "Thanks for making the time, [Name]. Here's how this will go: I'll tell you about Vanguard and the role, ask you a few questions, then we'll do a quick role-play where I play a business owner. At the end you can ask me anything. Sound good?"

### 2:00 – 6:00 · What Vanguard is and what the role is (be straight about pay)

> "Vanguard builds websites for local US businesses: salons, gyms, trades, auto shops. Our setters cold call businesses that don't have a website or have a weak one. Before anyone calls, we've already built that business a demo site. The setter's one job is to book a 15–30 minute call between the owner and me. I run that call and I build the site.
>
> "The pay is commission only. You earn $100 for every website project that closes from a call you booked, and once you hit 8 closed deals in a month, every deal that month pays $125. Our builds start at $997. You're paid after the client's payment clears, every two weeks. If a client is refunded within 60 days, that commission comes back off the next payout.
>
> "I want to be honest about what that means: there's no base pay and no pay per booked call. Your first month will usually be lower while you learn, and your earnings depend on calls that close. At full target a setter makes around $1,700 a month, but that is the target, not a promise.
>
> "You'd work as an independent contractor, at least 4 hours a day during US business hours."

Then ask: **"Does that structure work for you?"** If the answer is a hesitant no, end politely here. It saves you both time.

### 6:00 – 13:00 · Questions (pick 5–6)

| Ask | What a good answer sounds like | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| "Tell me about a job where you had to talk to strangers all day." | Specific job, specific situation, what they learned | Vague, no examples at all |
| "Most calls end in a no, often a rude one. Tell me about a time you kept going after a lot of rejection." | Real story; talks about what they did next | "That never bothers me" with nothing behind it |
| "What hours can you reliably work, and what could get in the way?" | Concrete hours, honest about constraints | "Anytime!" with no detail, or clashes with US hours |
| "Walk me through your setup: where you'd call from, headset, internet." | Quiet room, headset, backup internet or a plan | Café, phone speaker, shared noisy room |
| "Why commission-only, and why now?" | Understands the trade-off, has a goal | Expects quick big money; or clearly needs a guaranteed wage right now (be kind, it's not a fit) |
| "An owner asks you, 'Is this a scam?' What do you say?" | Honest: who we are, what it costs if they go ahead, no pressure | Dodges, invents something, "say it's free" |
| "An owner says 'don't call me again.' What do you do?" | Stop, apologise, add to do-not-call list the same day | Tries another pitch, or "call back next week" |
| "If a front-desk person won't put you through, what would you try?" | Ask for the best time to reach the owner, call back | Pretend to be a customer or a friend |
| "What do you do when you don't know an answer on a call?" | "I'd say I'll find out, or that the owner will cover it on the call" | Makes something up |

The two honesty questions (scam / don't call me) matter more than experience. A setter who bends the truth puts your phone numbers, your A2P registration and your reputation at risk.

### 13:00 – 21:00 · Role-play (the most important 8 minutes)

> "Let's do a quick role-play. I'm Mike, I own Mike's Auto Repair in Columbus, Ohio. 4.7 stars, 89 reviews, no website. You've already built me a demo. Call me, and try to book me onto a call with the closer. Ready? Ring ring."

**Round 1 (3 min).** Play a busy but fair owner. Throw these in order:
1. Pick up with "Mike's Auto, this is Mike." Let them open.
2. After the demo line: **"How much is this gonna cost me?"**
3. After their answer: **"Can't you just send it to me?"**
4. If they're still going: **"I get all my work from word of mouth anyway."**

**Feedback (1 min).** Give exactly one piece of feedback, the most useful one, kindly:
- "Slow down. After 'is that right?', stop and wait for my answer."
- "When I asked the price, give the ballpark straight away, then go back to booking."
- "Offer me two specific times instead of asking when I'm free."

**Round 2 (3 min).** Run it again from the top with the same objections.

**What you're scoring:** not the first attempt. **Did they actually change what you told them to change in round 2?** That's coachability, and it predicts success better than anything else in this interview.

### 21:00 – 24:00 · Their questions

Answer straight. Common ones:
- *"When do I get paid?"* Every two weeks, for payments that cleared in that period, by [Wise / PayPal / bank].
- *"Where do leads come from?"* We provide them, already researched, DNC-scrubbed and with a demo built.
- *"Is there a base?"* No. Commission only, $100–$125 per closed deal.
- *"Can I work from my phone?"* Calls are made through GHL on a laptop with a headset.

### 24:00 – 25:00 · Close

> "Thanks, [Name]. I'm finishing interviews this week and I'll message you by [day] either way."

Always give a date and always follow up, including with people you don't hire.

## 5. The scorecard

Fill this in **straight after** each interview, before the next one.

| Area | 1 (no) | 3 (ok) | 5 (strong) | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Voice & clarity** | Hard to follow | Clear but flat | Clear, warm, natural pace | /5 |
| **Coachability** (round 2 vs round 1) | Ignored the feedback | Partly applied it | Fully applied it, better overall | /5 |
| **Honesty & judgment** (scam / don't-call answers) | Would bend the truth | Unsure but honest | Clear, honest, calm | /5 |
| **Resilience** | No evidence | Some evidence | Real story of pushing through rejection | /5 |
| **Reliability** (hours, setup, punctuality) | Clashes or unreliable | Workable | Solid hours, good setup, early to interview | /5 |
| **Total** | | | | **/25** |

**Hire if:** 18+ total, **no 1s anywhere**, and **coachability 4 or 5**.
**Automatic no:** any answer suggesting they'd lie to a prospect or ignore a do-not-call request, regardless of total.

## 6. Stage 4 — Offer, rejection and paperwork

> **Message: offer**
>
> Hi [Name], I'd like to offer you the appointment setter role at Vanguard.
>
> Quick recap: commission only, $100 for every project that closes from a call you book, $125 on all deals in any month you close 8+. Paid every two weeks after the client's payment clears; 60-day clawback on refunds. Independent contractor, at least 4 hours a day in US business hours.
>
> If you're in, I'll send the contractor agreement today, and your start date is [date]. Can you confirm by [day]?
>
> [Your name], Vanguard

> **Message: not this time**
>
> Hi [Name], thanks for interviewing with Vanguard. I've decided to go with other candidates for this round, but I appreciated your time. Best of luck, and I'll keep your details in case anything opens up.

**Paperwork before day 1 (all signed, nothing verbal):**
- [ ] **Independent contractor agreement**: role, commission plan (attach `SETTER_PAY_PLAN.md` §8 summary), payment terms and clawback, confidentiality (leads, client data, scripts), no sub-contracting, either side can end it with notice.
- [ ] **Compliance acknowledgment**: DNC scrub before every dial; calling hours 8am–9pm in the lead's time zone; texts only after a verbal yes, logged in GHL; no pretexts (fake referrals, posing as a customer, claiming a demo exists when it doesn't); "don't call me" → internal do-not-call list the same day. Breaking these = no commission on that deal and possible termination (`SETTER_PAY_PLAN.md` §4).
- [ ] **Tax form**: W-9 for US-based setters, W-8BEN for setters outside the US. Keep on file; don't put tax IDs in Slack or email threads.
- [ ] **Payout details**: Wise, PayPal or bank (whatever you'll actually use every two weeks).

Get the contractor agreement checked by a lawyer once. Commission-only pay for US-based contractors carries classification and minimum-wage risk (see `SETTER_PAY_PLAN.md` §7).

**Accounts (each setter gets their own login; never share yours):**
- [ ] GHL user with the **setter** role only (their opportunities, the dialer, conversations, calendars; no settings, billing or exports)
- [ ] Slack (or Discord) as **Setter (trainee)**
- [ ] Their own Calendly access, or the shared booking link for the closer's calendar
- [ ] Shared folder with the playbook, training page, script PDF and industry playbooks

## 7. Stage 5 — The first two weeks

Week 1 follows `DISCORD_ONBOARDING.md` §3 day by day. The short version:

| Day | What happens | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| **Day 1** | 45-min welcome call. Walk through the Setter Playbook, the stack (GHL → Calendly → Slack), and where everything lives. They read the setting call script and watch training modules 01–03. | Logged into everything; asks 3 questions about the script |
| **Day 2** | Compliance training and quiz (DNC, hours, consent, no pretexts). **Nothing else until this is passed.** | Passes the quiz; signs the acknowledgment if not already done |
| **Day 3** | 10 role-plays (with you or another setter): every opener type, the 1–10 question, five objections, two-slot booking, commitment, texting consent. | Passes 3 role-plays in a row against the scorecard |
| **Day 4** | First live dials, 2-hour block, you listening in. Small list (40–50 leads). | Can run the full call without the PDF in front of them |
| **Day 5** | Second supervised block; review 3 recordings together. | Certification checklist reviewed |

**What to say on the welcome call (Day 1):**

> "Welcome in. Here's the only thing that matters in this job: a confirmed call with me, booked while you're still on the phone with the owner. Everything we train is in service of that.
>
> "Three rules I never bend on: we only call scrubbed numbers in calling hours, we only text people who said yes to texts, and we never say anything that isn't true: no fake referrals, no pretending, and we never say a demo exists unless it does. Everything else, I'll coach you on.
>
> "Your first week is training and supervised calling. Your first bookings will feel slow. That's normal. I'll review recordings with you twice a week, and you can message me on Slack any time you're stuck."

**Daily rhythm from week 2:**
- **Start of day** (Slack): hours you'll dial today, your target bookings.
- **End of day** (Slack, from GHL numbers): dials, pickups, real conversations, booked, one call you want feedback on.
- **Twice a week:** 15-minute call review with you, one recording each.
- **Weekly:** leaderboard and one script tweak.

**Day-14 review (15 minutes):**

| Signal by day 14 | What to do |
|---|---|
| Booking calls, following the script, rules clean | Certify (if not already) and keep going |
| Lots of dials, few bookings, but improving week on week | Coach on the specific gap (opener, booking ask); review again at day 30 |
| Low dial volume, missed check-ins, or not applying feedback | Part ways kindly. Commission-only setters who aren't dialing in week 2 rarely start in week 4 |
| Any compliance breach | Back to trainee + retraining, or end the contract if it was deliberate |

Ramp targets for week 2 should come from your first real numbers. As a starting point: **at least 4 hours of dialing a day and 1–2 bookings a day by the end of week 2.** Adjust once you have two weeks of data.

> **Message: parting ways**
>
> Hi [Name], thanks for the work these last two weeks. I don't think the role is the right fit right now, so I'm going to end the contract as of [date]. Any commission on deals from your bookings that close within the agreement's window will still be paid as agreed. I've removed your access to GHL and Slack today. I wish you the best.

**Offboarding (same day):** remove GHL, Slack and Calendly access; reassign their open leads; note any pending commissions; keep the signed documents on file.

## 8. One-page checklist

- [ ] Phones, DNC scrub, contract + payment link live (or start date set for when they are)
- [ ] Job post has an instruction to follow ("start with the word Vanguard")
- [ ] Screen applications (hours, English, setup, followed instruction)
- [ ] Send voice-note screen; pass the best 3–5
- [ ] 25-min interview: open → role & honest pay → questions → 2-round role-play → their questions → date
- [ ] Score immediately; hire on 18+/25, no 1s, coachability 4+
- [ ] Offer message → contractor agreement + compliance acknowledgment + tax form + payout details
- [ ] Own GHL setter login, Slack, Calendly, docs folder
- [ ] Week 1 training → certification
- [ ] Day-14 review; keep, coach, or part ways
