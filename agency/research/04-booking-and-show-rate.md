# 04: Booking the call live, and show rate (YouTube; Instagram attempted)

Researched 2026-10-15. Scope: how setters book the demo/fit call on a cold call (moving to the ask, two time slots, confirming the invite on the phone, commitment, handing off to the closer) and how to keep no-shows down with local-business owners.

## How this was verified (read first)

- Every YouTube item below was checked with oEmbed (`youtube.com/oembed`), which confirmed the title and channel. Dates come from the watch page's `dateText` where it loaded. YouTube later started bot-blocking the watch pages, so some dates are marked "not confirmed".
- Transcripts are YouTube auto-captions, downloaded with yt-dlp. **Quotes are copied from those captions.** I removed only filler ("um", "uh") and stutters, and marked any cut with "…". Auto-captions can mishear words, so check a line against the video before it goes into training material.
- **Instagram: no item is verified.** Instagram search through the web tool returned only profile pages (setter-for-hire accounts and agency profiles). Reels can't be fetched without logging in, so I could not confirm any reel's content. Nothing from Instagram is quoted here. The local-agency creators below (Abou Toure, Kai Stone, Pavlo) point viewers to their Instagram, so a manual check of those accounts would be the next step.
- **The 1–10 "intent" scale:** I could not find a verified video in which a setter asks "1–10, what's keeping it from a 9 or 10?" on a cold call. The closest verified material is listed under "Related to the 1–10 scale" below.

## 2. Per-source notes

### 1. Kai Stone: web-design agency, live calls to an Austin plumber and handyman
- **The setup is nearly identical to ours.** He builds a GoHighLevel template site in advance, then opens the call with "I basically built you a whole website completely for free."
- **The ask (quote):** "would you open to like getting on a Zoom call later today or tomorrow and I could just maybe show it to you? And if you like it, you can keep it. If not, you can tell me to… go play in trains."
- **Two-slot ask (quote):** "I have 3:00 and 5:00 p.m." The prospect was travelling in another state, so he worked out the time zone on the call ("12:00 p.m. California time").
- **Lets the owner pick the channel for the link (quote):** "Do you want me to just text it to you or do you want me to shoot you an email?" Owner: "text is easier for me." Then: "I could just text the same number I just called from?"
- **Keeps the time small (quote):** "15 minutes tops maybe, probably 10." On the handyman call, he offered to pull more photos from the Google profile onto the demo before the meeting. The prospect then said, "I'll put a little alarm on my phone."
- **Principle (quote):** "The only thing you are trying to do with that cold call is to get them on the Zoom appointment… You're not trying to sell the product yet. You are selling the Zoom call."
- **Claimed:** 3–5 meetings a day from 6–8 hours of calling; "$319,000 a month" agency (claimed, Kai Stone, same video).
- **Red flag (avoid):** his opener is a **made-up referral**: "my friend Julie Becker actually… my mom's friend kind of gave me your number." He says himself: "You can argue if it's ethical or not." He also presents himself as "a web design student… part of like a school thing." Do not copy either line unless it is true. His own clean alternative: quote a real Google review the business received.

### 2. Abou Toure: live web-design bookings (one by a beginner reading his script)
- **Opener and ask (quote):** "I noticed you didn't have a website, so I just went ahead and built you one from scratch if you just have five 10 minutes to see it."
- **Leaves the owner a way out (quote):** "If you like it, we can publish it. If you don't, I'll go kick rocks."
- **Open choice, then narrowing (quotes):** "Like Tuesday or Wednesday?" then "Does it work before 12 or after 12?" then "let's do a 1 then."
- **Captures and reads back the address live (quotes):** "What email would be the best to send you the Google Meet link to?" and "Can you repeat that again? You cut off for a sec." In the first call he also read the email back from his notes: "The email I have here is Brandon at…"
- **Commitment line, used twice (quote):** "Just a heads up. I've spent quite a bit of time working on this website, so I just want to confirm that you're sure you can make it at noon so we don't waste each other's time." The prospect reconfirmed: "Yeah, noon Tuesday should work." **This line is true for us, because we really have built the demo.**
- **Same-day close:** the first prospect agreed to a call about 15 minutes later ("I'll be waiting in the room").
- **Check before using:** he told a prospect who asked for a link, "it's actually like hosted on my laptop since it's not published yet." Use that only if it is literally true. Otherwise say the honest reason: you want to walk them through it.

### 3. Abou Toure: "Proven Script" (mock-up-first method)
- **Script (quote):** "Hey, I just found you guys on Google. Phenomenal reviews by the way, and I noticed you didn't have a website. I just built you a completely new website from scratch. Do you maybe have 10, five minutes later in the day for me to show it to you? If you like it, fantastic. I'll go ahead and publish it for you. If not, no worries."
- **Warns against discount openers (paraphrase):** leading with "25% discount" attracts price shoppers. Show the work rather than describing it.
- **Claimed:** "maybe… seven times more positive response rate" and "around one out of three people who actually hop on sales call with you are actually going to end up buying" (claimed, Abou Toure, same video). Both are unverified.

### 4. Thomas Gonnet: SMMA calls to US med spas
- **Ask (quote):** "we can schedule a quick Zoom call and I can show you sort of in screen share how the process works and what that looks like for some of our current clients." Then "does 11 work" and "I'll send you the confirmation for that… do you have an email where I can send that to."
- **After the session (paraphrase):** he sends confirmations for that day's three Zoom bookings right away, and logs callbacks for owners who asked for them.
- **Claimed:** about a "16% chance that I reach the owner", and "20 to 30 percent of the time when I do reach the business owner I'm able to close them into a meeting" (claimed, Gonnet).
- Note: his offer promises "at least 15 prepaid… appointments… or your money back". That is not relevant to us; don't borrow guarantee language we can't back.

### 5. 30 Minutes to President's Club: Cold Call Masterclass
- The description says the live calls are real but the **prospect voices were re-created with AI** for privacy.
- **Gate before booking logistics (quote):** "I'm not saying, can you pull up your calendar now? I'm not saying, 'Are you free Friday at 2 or 3?' I'm saying, 'Was this even interesting?' Because if it was, then we can get into meeting booking logistics."
- **"Sell the test drive, not the car" (quote):** "A cold call is not the place that you convince someone to buy your product. A cold call is where you create interest in a meeting."
- **Two-slot ask that names who will be on the call (quote):** "why don't we do 9:00 a.m. with Nick and then our lead sales rep, McKenna, on Tuesday the 13th or on Wednesday the 14th?" This is a clean pattern for introducing our closer (the owner) in the ask.
- **Bad-timing exit (quote):** "I'm going to send an email and a calendar hold… it'll be a tentative. Doesn't count as a meeting." He tracks it separately from booked meetings.
- **Claimed:** the script books meetings "on 1 out of every 3 connects" (claimed, 30MPC video description).

### 6. Jeb Blount (Sales Gravy): calling home-services and trades owners
- **"How much does it cost?" moved to the meeting (quote):** "that's exactly why I want to get together with you in an appointment when it's convenient so that I can learn a little bit more about your business… How about we get together on Thursday? What's the best time for you?"
- **"I'm busy" (quote):** "that's exactly why I called because in your business, I knew you were going to be busy. And all I'm looking for is a time that's more convenient for you." Also, paraphrase: owners who say "busy" are overwhelmed right now and aren't thinking about next Thursday.
- **For trades (paraphrase):** call early, before crews go out. He suggests trying 6:30–8:00 a.m., aiming for two first-time appointments a day, and offering to bring lunch or coffee to an in-person meeting. **See the Avoid list on calling hours.**
- **Stat caution:** the rep makes up "25% higher profit jobs" on the spot and Jeb says "Perfect". Only use stats you can source.

### 7. Matt Macnamara with a guest cold-calling coach
- **Moving from discovery to the ask (quote):** "don't forget to bring them back to the reason for your call… hey you know what Matt this is actually why I was calling you would be a terrible idea if instead of continuing this on a cold call out of the blue right now we found 30 minutes where I could properly set up and prep… worst case scenario you'll walk away with more knowledge… you know I have my calendar open how's Friday looking for you."
- **"Go for no" phrasing (quote):** "would it be insane if we found 30 minutes sometime on Friday…"
- **Show-rate principle (quote):** "you give them something of value they're going to show up to that call and that's the key to getting your show rates up is you want to offer them something for the time not just a demo." The speakers credit this to Anthony Iannarino.

### 8. Sales Feed: Cold Calling for Beginners

- **Confirming live (quote):** "if they say yes then book the meeting get their email or confirm their email while you're on the phone with them and send them a calendar invite and make sure it comes through you've come so far you don't want to lose them because you had their email address wrong and then send them an email to confirm what the meeting is going to be about and what you heard on the cold call"

### 9. FulltimeDragon: SMMA no-show prevention (small channel)
- **The person who owns the next call phones right after booking (quote):** "whoever's owning that specific next phone call calls that person immediately as soon as… the call is booked and confirms and say hey nice to meet you… look forward to chatting with you and see you on X date at X time." The reason he gives: people show up when "they know that somebody is waiting for us."
- **Get the cell number (quote):** "if we're calling an office line or we don't have their cell have the salesperson ask them and say just to confirm like your cell I have your cell here and it's this number right"
- **Cadence (paraphrase):** text confirmation right away; a 24-hour confirmation call ("I know… life happens… we'll see you tomorrow"); if the calendar invite isn't accepted, ask them to accept it; a personalised video on the morning of the call; a call 2 hours before. He says the 2-hour call once let him reschedule rather than lose a demo.

### 10. Jordan Park (RevPilot): 9 steps to fix show rate
- **Confirmation framed as a curiosity question (quotes):** "Hey, just saw your time come through. Curious. If we solved one thing first for you, what would change the most in the next 30 days?" Voice-note version: "Saw your booking come through. Excited to connect. Quick question for you. What's the part of this that stresses you out the most? That way I can come prepared to the call."
- **Cadence (quote):** "At booking, you anchor the appointment. So at 24 hours, you resurface attention. At 2 hours, you jog memory… And then at 10 minutes, you close the gap and get them in the actual room."
- **Handoff (paraphrase plus quote):** most setter-to-closer handoffs feel like "a baton toss". In a group chat, "the setter introduces the closer in front of the prospect with context and the closer replies quickly with something specific."
- **Warns against double booking** as a fix: it "makes your process feel less premium and it hides the real issue."
- **Claimed:** going from 50% to 70% show rate; ideal-client show rates of "70 to 80%"; group-chat handoff "can bump your close rate by 10 to 15 points" (claimed, Jordan Park).

### 11. Ste McGrath: pre-call system
- **Personal pre-call voice note from the person taking the call (quote):** "Hey, John, just leaving you a message to let you know that I've got your call booked in at 2 p.m. on Thursday. I was just looking through your form…" He follows with a text asking "You've got the link right?" and always ends on a question: "if they respond to that, I know we're good for the showing up."
- **Also (paraphrase):** a thank-you page with a short framing video ("this is what's going to happen… on the call"), short FAQ videos, testimonials, and SMS reminders. He suggests a booking window of about 48 hours.
- **Claimed:** show rate from "30 to 50%" up to "60 to 70%", and "80s plus" for warm traffic (claimed, Ste McGrath).

### 12. Mark Coles: fixing show-up rate (fitness-coach audience)
- **Booking window (quote):** "you should never open your calendar for more than 3 days at a time… Ideally, we'd like it to be 2 days."
- **Voice note within an hour asking for a reply (quote):** "Could you do me a favor? Please drop me a message back and just confirm that you have the time in your diary and it's confirmed for this time and this date."
- **Pre-call homework, related to the 1–10 scale (quote):** "score yourself out of 10 for each one. And then come on the call and explain to me where you feel that you are."
- **Avoid (quote):** "if I don't hear from you 12 hours before the call, I will have to cancel the appointment and make the appointment available for somebody who is ready to actually commit to their goals." That is pressure and implied scarcity; see the Avoid list.

### 13. Lavender ("Will"): 3 simple steps to reduce no-shows
- **Claimed (Lavender poll):** the top reason buyers no-show is "no clear value or agenda for that meeting". Second: "people were feeling pressured into taking the meeting". Quote: "Do not force meetings onto prospects. It hurts you more than it helps you."
- **Agenda email (quote):** "Hey name, looking forward to our 30 minute meeting on time and date. The agenda for that meeting is as follows… attending that meeting will be yourself, myself, and my SDR Sam. Please let me know if there's anything you'd like to add to the agenda."
- **Invite acceptance (quote):** "make sure that they actually accept that calendar invite. In my experience, if someone hasn't accepted a calendar invite, then it's very unlikely that they're actually going to show up." His trick: add "please confirm:" to the start of the invite title and re-send the update.
- **Booking window (paraphrase):** book within a week of agreement, two weeks at most. Further out, momentum dies.
- **Reminders (paraphrase):** one automatic reminder on the day of the meeting.
- **No-show recovery (quotes):**
  - At 5 minutes: "Ready when you are" plus the link.
  - At 10 minutes, call: "hey, it's Will, we had a meeting this time, are you still good for it?"
  - Voicemail: "We scheduled some time to meet today to discuss blank. Let me know if something came up."
  - Stay in the room for the full slot.
  - Final email: "Hey name, assuming something came up, let me know if you'd like to rebook this. All good if not, but I hope everything's okay."
  - Principle: "no guilt tripping".

### 14. Sabo Nagy: when a prospect doesn't show
- **Timed script (quotes):**
  - At 2 minutes, call, double-dial, and text: "hey first name Sabo with scaling room here. I just emailed you a link to join me on Google meets. Speak to you shortly."
  - At 5 minutes: "Are you delayed? Can you make it?"
  - At 10 minutes: "I'll wait in the meeting room for 5 minutes. Are you still coming?"
  - At 15 minutes, close the room and reply: "Looks like you couldn't make it for this one. That's all right… here's a reschedule link."
  - Policy: "We always allow one reschedule." He also keeps a no-show tracking sheet.
- **Claimed:** the routine "will pretty much guarantee that you have zero no shows". This is an unsupported claim.
- **Avoid:** his 7-step no-show follow-up includes "We've got a few slots left this month", "the last time we're offering bonus", and "are you getting them or is your competitor?". Those are generic scarcity and fear-of-missing-out templates; see the Avoid list.

### 15. Build That Agency (Adam McLaughlin): goal of a web-design cold call
- **Low-friction ask (quote):** "I don't need anything from you… what's the best email address for me to send your free evaluation". Offering something free and asking only for an email lowers the bar to say yes.
- **Bonus tip (paraphrase):** warm the cold call by sponsoring a trade association or newsletter first, so you can say "you may have seen our ad in…". Use only if true.

## 3. Upgrades to our script

Our current flow: opener → discovery (including the 1–10 question) → reason for the call → objections → set the call live → close out. Below are concrete lines for each booking step. Where a line is adapted rather than quoted, it says "adapted".

### (a) From discovery to the ask
1. **Gate on interest first, then do logistics.** After the reason for the call, ask a yes/no interest question before any times: *"Was that even interesting, seeing what a site for [Business] could look like?"* (adapted from 30MPC #5: "Was this even interesting?"). Only offer slots on a yes.
2. **Use their 1–10 answer as the bridge** (adapted from Matt Macnamara #7: "this is actually why I was calling…"): *"You said a [7], and the thing keeping it from a 9 is [their words]. That's actually why I called. Rather than describe it over the phone, would it be crazy to grab 15 minutes with [Owner], the guy who built it, so you can see it on screen? Worst case, you walk away with a free look at what [Business] could have online."*
3. **Sell the call, not the site** (Kai Stone #1: "You are selling the Zoom call"; 30MPC #5: "Sell the test drive, not the car"). Price questions go to the call, but keep our honest ballpark from discovery. Jeb's wording works (#6): *"Good question, and that's exactly why it's worth 15 minutes: [Owner] will show you what's built and give you a straight number."*
4. **Leave them a real way out** (Abou Toure #2/#3; Kai Stone #1): *"If you like it, great. If not, no hard feelings."*

### (b) The two-slot ask
1. **Two specific slots, and name the closer** (30MPC #5: "9:00 a.m. with Nick and then our lead sales rep, McKenna, on Tuesday the 13th or on Wednesday the 14th?"; Kai Stone #1: "I have 3:00 and 5:00 p.m."). Adapted: *"[Owner] has [tomorrow at 10] or [Thursday at 2]. Which is better for you?"*
2. **If neither works, narrow it down rather than reopening the calendar** (Abou Toure #2): *"No problem. Is earlier or later in the week better? Before noon or after?"*
3. **Book close in time.** Aim for within 48–72 hours. Mark Coles (#12) says never more than 3 days out; Ste McGrath (#11) about 48 hours; Lavender (#13) a week at most. Same-day is fine if they're keen: Kai and Abou both booked calls hours or minutes later.
4. **Check the time zone out loud** when the slot is set (Kai Stone #1, where the owner was in another state): *"That's 2 p.m. your time in [City], right?"*
5. **Trades and busy owners** (Jeb #6): *"Totally get it. That's exactly why I'm not asking for now, just a time that suits you better."* Never stack a third or fourth slot. Two, then narrow.

### (c) Confirming the invite on the call
1. **Capture and read back the email; send the invite while they're still on the phone** (Sales Feed #8: "confirm their email while you're on the phone with them and send them a calendar invite and make sure it comes through"; Abou Toure #2: "What email would be the best to send you the Google Meet link to?" plus "Can you repeat that again?"). Adapted: *"I'm sending it now. It'll come from [sender] with the subject 'Your [Business] website preview with [Owner]'. Can you see it?"* Ask them to accept it if they can (Lavender #13).
2. **Ask which channel they prefer for the link; that is also asking permission to text** (Kai Stone #1: "Do you want me to just text it to you or do you want me to shoot you an email?"). Adapted: *"Is it OK if I also text the link to this number, or is there a better cell?"* (FulltimeDragon #9: "I have your cell here and it's this number right"). Record the yes in the CRM.
3. **Give them the length and the agenda in one breath** (Kai Stone #1: "15 minutes tops"; Lavender #13 agenda email). Adapted: *"It's 15–20 minutes: [Owner] shows you the demo, asks a couple of questions about how customers find you, and gives you a straight price. If it's not for you, you say so."*
4. **Leave the invite title and body ready-made** (Lavender #13): date, time, time zone, length, link, the closer's name and photo, the agenda in three bullets, and a link to reschedule.

### (d) Commitment (honest)
1. **The demo is real, so say so** (Abou Toure #2, adapted): *"Just so you know, [Owner] has already put real time into building [Business]'s demo, so I want to make sure [Thursday at 2] actually works for you. If anything comes up, just text this number and we'll move it, no problem."* The easy reschedule matters: it keeps the line honest and avoids the pressure Lavender links to no-shows.
2. **Hand them a reason to show up** (Matt Macnamara #7: "offer them something for the time"): *"Even if you don't go ahead, you'll get a quick look at how you show up on Google versus [competitor type] nearby."* Only promise what the closer will actually deliver.
3. **A small piece of homework that matches our 1–10 question** (Jordan Park #10; Mark Coles #12 "score yourself out of 10"). Adapted: *"Before the call, have a think about the one thing you'd want a website to do for you: more calls, bookings, looking legit? [Owner] will start there."*
4. **Repeat it back and end on a question** (Abou Toure #2): *"So that's [day] at [time] your time, with [Owner], on [Zoom/Meet]. Does that still work?"* Wait for a spoken "yes".
5. **Handoff to the closer.** Name the closer in the ask (30MPC #5). After the call, the closer sends a personal text or voice note the same day (FulltimeDragon #9: "whoever's owning that specific next phone call"; Jordan Park #10 group-chat introduction). Adapted setter text, once they've agreed to texts: *"Hi [Name], it's [Setter]. Here's [Owner], who built your demo; he'll see you [Thu 2pm]."* Closer reply: *"Hi [Name], looking forward to it. I've already added your Google photos to the demo. Anything specific you want me to look at?"* The setter's CRM note to the closer should carry the 1–10 answer and "what's keeping it from a 9/10" in the owner's own words.

### (e) Reminders and no-show recovery
1. **Cadence (touches only by channels they agreed to):**
   - At booking: the invite plus one confirmation (Ste McGrath #11; Jordan Park #10).
   - Same day: a personal voice note or text from the closer, ending on a question such as *"You got the link OK?"* (Ste McGrath #11). A reply is a strong sign they'll show.
   - 24 hours before: a short confirmation (FulltimeDragon #9; Jordan Park #10).
   - Morning of, or 2 hours before: a call or text (FulltimeDragon #9; Jordan Park #10).
   - Keep it to that. Lavender (#13) recommends no more than one automated reminder on the day.
2. **If the invite isn't accepted:** re-send it with "Please confirm:" added to the start of the title (Lavender #13).
3. **No-show script (a blend of Lavender #13 and Sabo Nagy #14):**
   - At 2–5 minutes, text: *"Hi [Name], [Owner] here. I'm on the call now, link: [link]."*
   - At 10 minutes, call once and leave this voicemail: *"Hi [Name], [Owner] from Vanguard. We had a few minutes booked to look at your website demo. No worries if something came up; just text me a better time."*
   - Stay in the room for the full slot (Lavender #13).
   - Afterwards: *"Assuming something came up, all good. Want to grab another time? [link]"* (Lavender #13; Sabo Nagy #14).
4. **Policy:** allow at least one easy reschedule (Sabo Nagy #14). Log no-shows and reschedules by source and niche to see whether the problem is the list, the niche or the setter (Jordan Park #10 on segmenting show rate). No guilt-tripping, no "cancelling your spot".

## 4. Avoid list

| Tactic | Seen in | Why avoid | Do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Made-up referral ("my friend Julie Becker… my mom's friend gave me your number") | Kai Stone #1, who admits "You can argue if it's ethical or not" | Deceptive. It breaks trust with an owner as soon as they ask who Julie is, and conflicts with our honest pattern-interrupt opener | A real hook: their actual Google reviews, "noticed you don't have a website", the real demo |
| Persona lies ("I'm a web design student… part of a school thing") or reasons that aren't true ("it's hosted on my laptop") | Kai Stone #1; Abou Toure #2 | Misleading; easy to uncover | Say who we are (a small US agency) and the real reason we prefer a screen-share |
| Fake scarcity or urgency ("a few slots left this month", "last time we're offering bonus", "the calendar is almost full") | Sabo Nagy #14 follow-up templates | Lies unless literally true. Small owners compare notes and will catch it | If capacity really is limited, say exactly what's true ("[Owner] builds X sites a month, the next start date is …") |
| Cancellation threat or guilt ("I will have to cancel… make the appointment available for somebody who is ready to actually commit") | Mark Coles #12 | Pressure and shaming. Lavender (#13) found that feeling pressured is a top reason people no-show | A friendly confirmation, an easy reschedule, no threats |
| Guilt-tripping or punishing no-shows (including the joke about adding no-shows to drip campaigns "as punishment") | Lavender #13 warns against guilt and makes that joke | Destroys the chance to rebook | "Assuming something came up…" message, one rebook offer |
| Forcing the meeting / not taking "no" | Lavender #13 | Bookings made under pressure don't show and waste the closer's time | Ask "Was that even interesting?" first (30MPC #5). Let a "no" go politely |
| Texting or WhatsApp-ing reminders without consent; automated marketing texts | Kai, Abou, FulltimeDragon, Ste McGrath and Mark Coles all use text, WhatsApp or voice notes | US texting rules (TCPA and carrier A2P 10DLC registration) and state laws can apply, even to a sole trader's cell | Ask on the call ("OK if I text the link to this number?"), log the yes, keep texts to appointment logistics, honour STOP. Have counsel confirm the setup |
| Calling owners before 8 a.m. | Jeb Blount #6 suggests trying 6:30 a.m. for trades | Federal telemarketing rules limit calls to residential lines (which many trades owners' cells are) to 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time; some states are narrower | Call 8 a.m. or later, local to the prospect. Check state rules and the do-not-call (DNC) lists |
| Making up stats on the spot ("25% higher profit jobs") | Jeb Blount #6 (rep's stat, approved on air) | Unsubstantiated claims | Only stats we can source, or none |
| Guarantee language we can't back ("15 prepaid appointments… or your money back") | Thomas Gonnet #4 | Our offer is a $2,000 build, not a results guarantee | Promise only what's in the offer (live within 14 days of kick-off) |
| Double-booking the closer to make up for no-shows | Jordan Park #10 warns against it | Feels un-premium and hides the real problem | Fix the list, the confirmation and the reminders |
| Trusting creators' headline claims ("$319k/month", "zero no-shows", "7x response rate") | Kai Stone #1, Sabo Nagy #14, Abou Toure #3 | Unverified, and often used to sell courses or affiliate links | Track our own book rate, show rate and close rate by niche |

### Claimed stats (all unverified; for calibration only)
- 3–5 meetings a day from 6–8 hours of calling; "$319,000 a month" agency (Kai Stone #1)
- 2–3 meetings a day (Abou Toure #2); "seven times more positive response" and about 1 in 3 sales calls close (Abou Toure #3)
- About 16% of dials reach the owner; 20–30% of owner conversations book (Thomas Gonnet #4)
- 1 meeting per 3 connects (30MPC #5)
- Goal of 2 first-time appointments a day from about 25 early-morning dials (Jeb Blount #6)

- 50% rising to 70% show rate; ideal clients 70–80%; group-chat handoff adds 10–15 points to close rate (Jordan Park #10)

- "Zero no shows" (Sabo Nagy #14)
