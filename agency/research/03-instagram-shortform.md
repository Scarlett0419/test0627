# 03 — Instagram / short-form creators: cold call scripts for selling websites to local businesses

Researched 2026-10-15. Scope: creators who post short-form content (Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube) about cold calling local businesses to sell websites, and the scripts they give away.

**Read this first: Instagram post content could not be read.** Search engines here return Instagram *profile* pages only, never `/p/` or `/reel/` pages. Instagram's login wall and rate limits (HTTP 429, then "Login • Instagram") blocked every post-level fetch. So no Reel caption or carousel is quoted in this file. What was verified:
- (a) Creators' Instagram handles and profile stats, from search-index snippets and one direct profile meta read.
- (b) The scripts those creators publish on other channels: YouTube descriptions, a public Google Doc and a public Google Drive PDF.

The platform each item was actually verified on is stated per row. See section 5.

## 2. Per-source notes

### 1. Kai Stone (@kaistone808): "How i book 3-5 meetings a day Cold Calling ($319,000/month web design agency - full script)"
- **Distribution:** the video description links the free Google Doc ("FULL COLD CALL SCRIPT"). He also sells via a GoHighLevel affiliate link and a Skool group, and the description gives his IG.
- **Opener (quoted):** "Hey is this (insert their name / business name)?" Then: "Hey what's up *their name*, my mom's friend Julie Becker referred me to you guys, she said you did super high quality work, the *niche* right?" **This is a fabricated referral. See the Avoid list.**
- **No-website hook (quoted):** "Yeah she told me to give you guys a call, said you guys don't have a website or a google page or anything like that?" His note: either they agree, or they say they have a Google page and you follow up "but you don't have a website?" Either way you are in conversation.
- **Framing (quoted):** "I just graduated college and I've started a little (agency)… Right now I'm doing (service) for super cheap with the hopes I might be able to get a testimonial from you… do you mind if I build something out for you to take a look at?"
- **Price answer (quoted):** "I cover the cost of the monthly hosting and the domain name, right now I'm just charging between $97-$497…" This does not fit Vanguard's $2,000 build.
- **Booking ask (quoted):**
  - "Even better than a call back let me just schedule into my calendar, give me one sec."
  - "How's 'x time' today, I also have 'x time' open tomorrow, what works better for you?"
  - No-show lock: "besides the off chance there's a zombie apocalypse at 'x appointment time' would there be any reason you wouldn't show up?"
  - Reason to attend: "at the very least you can learn a little bit about your business from an advertising perspective + see what some of your competitors are doing."
  - Low-effort framing: "All I would need from you is to fill out a basic form with some info about your business."
- **Objections (quoted / close paraphrase):**
  - "We don't need a website, we already have enough business." Answer: a site "allows you to charge more for the same service you're already doing… quote the overflow you get at a higher price."
  - "Too expensive." Answer: ask what a typical job is worth, then do "5th grade math" (one extra job a year pays for it). He closes with the joke "If I can't get you at least one job a year to 5x your return… I'd probably just go play in traffic."
- **Mindset (quoted):**
  - "the goal at the start of the call is just to get them not to hang up on you."
  - "The worst thing you can do is sound like you're a drone reading off a script."
- **Follow-up:** text a case study right after the call. Send 30–90 second selfie videos before the appointment. If a text goes unanswered: "Did you get a chance to watch that video?" / "?" / "Thoughts?"
- **Setter KPIs (claimed):**
  - "5 hours = (~150 calls a day!)"
  - "1-2 Booked appointments per day… 5-10 per week… 3-5 of these appointments should show up."
  - "I have made the majority of my closes off calling people back between 5-11 times."
- **Claimed results:** "$319,000/month web design agency" (video title).

### 2. Ernesto Lopez: "This COLD CALL SCRIPT makes me $3000/WEEK (SMMA Web Design)" plus the free PDF
This is the closest match to Vanguard's hook in the whole research set: the site is pre-built and the ask is a 5-minute Zoom.
- **Intro (quoted):** "Hey How's it Going?" … "Hey, this is [name], glad to finally be on the phone with you." The second line implies a prior relationship. See the Avoid list.
- **Pitch (quoted):** "I actually saw you guys on google and….. Honestly, I'm a fan of your work. So I took a big risk, I saw you don't have a website so I built you one. I want to show you it for 5 minutes and if you like it you can keep it."
- **Time objection (quoted):** "Perfect, I love that you're busy, and thats exactly why we already built the website out for you before we got on this call, So how about we do this, I can give you a call later on today when you're less busy and it will take no more than 5 minutes to show you it. and you can be honest with me if you don't like it i will just delete it and if you do like it then we can see about the next steps."
- **"Too much work already" (quoted):** "I love to hear that you're killing it in the industry. I'm sorry to inform you that this will 100% bring you new work and it will also help you automate… missed calls text backs, automatic booking…" The "100%" is a guarantee claim. See the Avoid list.
- **"Not interested" (quoted):** "we know that more than 700+ people are searching for [services] in your area and they are not finding you… there is only 2 reasons why you would not make 5 minutes… 1. You're comfortable with the amount of work… 2. or you think its gonna be too expensive or its a scam? so which one is it?"
- **"Next week" (quoted):** "The reason im calling specifically today is because we can only keep the website for a short period since it cost us to have it on our systems." This is manufactured urgency. See the Avoid list.
- **Booking (quoted):**
  - "what time would work best for you?"
  - "is that when you get out of work?"
  - "is there any reason you can think of why you would not be able to make it today at 6pm?"
  - "you should be receiving a text message with a Zoom link…"
  - "my name is Ernesto by the way, what was your name?"
- **Rules (quoted):** "Always confirm the appointment / Always get a name / Always send a zoom link while still on the call it increases show up rate by 50%." The 50% figure is claimed and unsourced.
- **Probing questions (quoted):**
  - "Hypothetically would this be your first time having a website?"
  - "Is there any reason why you have not gotten a website done yet?"
  - "How do you currently attract new customers or clients?"
  - "How do you currently manage your booked estimates and appointments?"
- **Notes (quoted):** "Always agree. (Perfect, I completely understand, You're absolutely right…)". "Do not speak to fast or too slow."
- **Claimed results:** "$3000/WEEK" (title).

## 3. Best short lines for Vanguard

These lines fit the offer: the demo is already built, the setter's only job is to book a 15–30 minute call with the owner, and the build costs $2,000 upfront. Lines marked *adapted* have been changed from the source to make them honest or to fit the offer.

**Openers / pattern interrupts**
1. "Hey, is this [owner name] with [business]?" — confirm the person first. Source: Kai Stone (pattern only; get the name from Google reviews).
2. *Adapted from Ernesto Lopez:* "I came across [business] on Google — [X] reviews, people clearly like your work — and noticed you don't have a website. So I took a bit of a risk and we built you one. I'd love to show it to you; it takes about 15 minutes, and if you don't like it, no hard feelings." Changes from the original: the honest "came across" replaces "glad to finally be on the phone with you", and a real review count replaces the bare "fan of your work".
3. Pavlo's framing, condensed: "I built it — want to take a look or not?" Use it as the pivot after the opener. It is a low-pressure yes/no.
4. *Adapted from Kai Stone:* "Am I right that it's just the Google page right now, no actual website?" This gets agreement early. If they say "we have Google," answer "right, but no site of your own?"

**One-line objection handlers**
- **"I'm busy."** "Totally, that's exactly why we built it before calling. Can I grab 15 minutes later today or tomorrow to walk you through it?" Source: Ernesto Lopez, *adapted* from 5 minutes to 15 minutes.
- **"We have enough work."** "Love that. A proper site usually lets you price the overflow higher instead of turning it away." Source: Kai Stone, *adapted*.
- **"Too expensive" / "How much?"** The setter should not quote or defend price. Ask "what's a typical job/ticket worth to you?", then say "[closer] goes over pricing on the call; it's a one-time build, and you'll see the actual site first." Source: Kai Stone's math idea, *adapted*. For low-ticket niches like barbers, the "one extra job pays for it" math is weak at $2,000, so don't force it.
- **"Send me a sample."** This is one of the objections Pavlo lists. Suggested reply: "Happy to — can I text you the link to the demo now while we're on the phone, and we'll book 15 minutes to walk through it?" Only text after they say yes.
- **"Not interested."** Use one honest question, then accept the no: "Fair enough — is it more the timing or that a website just isn't a priority?" This is *adapted* from Ernesto's two-reasons close, with the "or is it a scam?" pressure removed.

**Booking asks / no-show reducers**
- "Even better than a call back, let me just put it in the calendar — how's [time] today, or I've got [time] tomorrow?" Source: Kai Stone. Offer two options only.
- "Is that when you get off work?" … "Any reason you wouldn't be able to make [time]?" Source: Ernesto Lopez.
- "Besides the off chance of a zombie apocalypse, anything that'd stop you making [time]?" Source: Kai Stone. It works if it suits the setter's personality.
- "My name's [name] by the way — what was yours?" Source: Ernesto Lopez. Get the name before hanging up.
- "At the very least you'll see the site and what your competitors are doing online." Source: Kai Stone, *adapted*.
- After the call, send the confirmation and demo link (by text only with verbal permission). The day before, send a short personal video. Source: Kai Stone's follow-up playbook.

**Discovery questions the setter can ask in under 30 seconds** (Ernesto Lopez)
- "Would this be your first time having a website?"
- "Any reason you haven't gotten one done yet?"
- "How do new customers usually find you right now?"

## 4. Avoid list (deceptive or non-compliant tactics found in these sources)

| Tactic | Where seen | Why to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Fabricated referral**: "my mom's friend Julie Becker referred me to you" | Kai Stone script doc. His note describes how to handle prospects who ask who Julie Becker is. Pavlo's 2026 description mentions "referral-style openings" but the content is unverified. | A pretext call. The FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule bars misrepresentations in sales calls, and it damages trust in the closer's call. Never use it. |
| **False persona / price story**: "I just graduated college… doing it super cheap for a testimonial" | Kai Stone | A misrepresentation if untrue. It also clashes with Vanguard's $2,000 upfront price and will blow up on the close call. |
| **Implied prior relationship**: "glad to finally be on the phone with you" | Ernesto Lopez | Implies a prior connection that doesn't exist. Open honestly with "I came across your business on Google." |
| **Fake scarcity**: "we can only keep the website for a short period since it cost us to have it on our systems" | Ernesto Lopez | Manufactured urgency. Only state a real deadline, for example if demos really are taken down after X days. |
| **Guarantees**: "this will 100% bring you new work"; "If I can't get you at least one job a year to 5x your return…" | Ernesto Lopez; Kai Stone | Outcome guarantees the agency can't back. They create refund and dispute risk on a $2,000 upfront sale. |
| **Unverified stats on the phone**: "more than 700+ people are searching for [services] in your area" | Ernesto Lopez | Only quote a number pulled for that niche and city (e.g., a keyword tool). Otherwise it is a fabricated claim. |
| **Pressure after a clear no**: "too expensive or… a scam? so which one is it?"; calling back "until they tell you to go play in traffic" | Ernesto Lopez; Kai Stone (5–11 callbacks) | Ask one question, then respect the no. Honor any "don't call me" immediately: keep an internal do-not-call list (TSR/TCPA), scrub against the National DNC Registry, and call only between 8am and 9pm in the prospect's time zone. |
| **Texting without consent / cold SMS**: Zoom link "via text message"; "combining cold calling with cold SMS" | Ernesto Lopez; Pavlo (Jun 2026) | TCPA and A2P 10DLC risk. Text only after verbal "yes, text me" on the call, from a registered number. No bulk cold SMS. |
| **Caller-ID games**: "calling from a personal number… boosts pickup rates" | James (@coolcalling) | Fine only if the number is real and the setter answers it. Never spoof (Truth in Caller ID Act; TSR requires caller ID to be transmitted). |
| **AI voice agents cold-calling** | Seen in YouTube search titles (e.g., "This AI Voice Agent Cold Calls Businesses and Sells Them Websites") | The FCC's 2024 ruling treats AI-generated voices as "artificial" under the TCPA, so calls need prior express consent. Human setters only. |
| **Treating creator claims as benchmarks** | All the "$319K/mo", "$500K/mo", "3–5 meetings a day" titles | These are affiliate-funnel marketing (GoHighLevel links, DM-gated courses). Use Kai Stone's own KPI as a sanity check (~150 dials/day for 1–2 bookings, about half of which show) and Sam Robinson's 5,000 calls over 2 years as the realistic counterpoint. |

## 5. What could NOT be accessed, and why

- **Instagram Reels, posts and carousels: nothing at post level.**
  - The search tool's Instagram index only returned profile pages.
  - Instagram's logged-out pages returned a login wall. After a couple of profile reads, crawler-UA requests got "Login • Instagram".
  - The public profile JSON endpoint returned 401 ("Please wait a few minutes… require_login").
  - WebFetch returned HTTP 429.
  - Third-party viewers (imginn, picuki, pixwox, dumpor) were behind 403 or Cloudflare challenges.
  - So **no Reel captions or carousel scripts are quoted**, and the "steal my script" carousels the brief asked about could not be checked.
- **Instagram profile stats** come from search-engine snippets: @kaistone808, @pavels_life, @yurvywebsite, @aiw and @spencerreist. The exception is @abouxtoure, read directly from og:meta. @spence.nano, @_maxross1 / @maxevanross and @dreamofmike (Michael Vanhoutte) appear only in YouTube descriptions and are unconfirmed.
- **Gated scripts:**
  - Abou Toure and yurvy: "DM me on Instagram". Not done; that would need an account and contact with the creator.
  - Pavlo: behind a GoHighLevel affiliate signup.
  - Spencer Reist: behind a quiz funnel.
  - Max Ross: guide short link returned 403.
- **Spoken content of videos:** YouTube transcripts were blocked (bot check / "Precondition check failed"). TikTok pages render client-side and returned only page shells, and TikTok oEmbed gives captions only, which were hashtag-only. Spoken lines from the live calls are therefore **not** quoted. Only on-page descriptions and the two creator-published documents were read.
- **Method note:** besides WebSearch and WebFetch, read-only HTTP GETs were made with curl: YouTube oEmbed and page data, the public Google Doc export, the public Drive PDF, and Instagram profile meta. Nothing was posted, no accounts were used, and no repository files were touched.
