# 01 — YouTube: live recorded cold calls selling websites to local businesses

Research date: 2026-10-15. Scope: YouTube videos where creators cold call local businesses live (or play real recordings) to sell websites, ideally with a pre-built demo or "I built you a site" hook.

**How this was verified**
- Every video below was confirmed via YouTube oEmbed (title and channel) and/or yt-dlp metadata (upload date, duration, views, description, chapters).
- **Transcripts** are YouTube auto-captions pulled with yt-dlp. Quotes are copied from those captions, so they may have small transcription errors (names, filler words), but no words were added. Anything I didn't see verbatim is marked *(paraphrase)*.
- Three upload dates could not be retrieved because YouTube blocked further metadata requests (bot check). Those are marked "n/r".
- **Geography matters.** Several of the strongest call recordings come from Canada, the UK or Australia, and that is flagged per video. Only US calls should be treated as close analogues for Vanguard.

## 2. Per-video notes

### #1 Spencer Reist: "How to Book 3+ Meetings a Day Web Design Cold Calling (Proven Script)" (US)
- **Stated principles:** "one I never pitch the gatekeeper two sound like a real person not a robot and three these guys are busy so typically I'll just jump straight into the offer."
- **Opener (call 1, verbatim from captions):** "hey Victor this is Spencer um so the reason I'm calling I'm actually checking out your [business] here right now and I noticed you have some great reviews but didn't have a website so believe it or not I actually went ahead and built you a brand new beautiful website to help you get more jobs on Google and I was wondering if you had like 10 to 15 minutes tomorrow to jump on a quick video call to check it out"
- **Variant (call 2):** "I'm actually checking out your [business] and I noticed you didn't have a website so I actually built you uh a brand new beautiful one I was wondering if you'd be open to jumping on like a quick 10minute video call tonight or tomorrow to check it out" → Prospect: "yeah that'd be awesome"
- **Demo intro:** delivered in a single breath together with the meeting ask. There's no pitch before the ask.
- **Questions after booking (light discovery):**
  - "are most of your jobs right now just coming from word of mouth"
  - "I'm assuming you're able to take on more jobs though right"
  - "is your main Services painting or electrical or just both"
- **Meeting ask and close:**
  - Offers a specific time ("what's your evening look like tonight around like 6"), then accepts the prospect's counter.
  - Gets the email and sends an invite.
  - "do you get text at this number … I'll remind you over text as well"
- **Objections:**
  - Spouse answers, "we actually do have a website I think… send it to my husband's email". Spencer takes the email; this ended up only a partial booking.
  - Snowbird owner moving to Florida: Spencer pivots to a new Google Business Profile plus a site for the new area.
  - "how quickly can you get me the first page": "I don't want to make any false promises because to be honest a lot of it will come down to getting reviews"
- **Outcome:** 3 meetings booked on camera (Victor, Cody, Paul) plus 1 email follow-up.
- **Claimed:** "3+ meetings a day" (title).
- **Compliance notes:**
  - He asks before texting.
  - The opener is truthful *if* the site exists.
  - Refusing to promise rankings is good practice.

### #2 Abou Toure: "How To Book 3+ Web Design Meetings Per Day [Live Cold Calling]" (Canada)
- **Opener (verbatim):** "Hey, I'm located in South Calgary. I just found you guys on Google. Fantastic reviews, by the way. And I noticed you didn't have a website, so I just went ahead and built you one from scratch if you just have five 10 minutes to see it."
- **Objection "not super interested in a website":** "I totally hear you. And most my clients say the exact same thing before they actually see the website … Like I said, it's already built. If you have 5 10 minutes, even right now, I'll just share my screen"
- **Risk reversal:** "If you like it, we can publish it. If you don't, I'll go kick rocks."
- **Objection "send me the link":** "Ah, I really wish I could do that, but it's actually like hosted on my laptop since it's not published yet. Um, so the only way … is like I just share my screen". **Flag:** this is likely a convenient fiction, since most builders produce a shareable preview.
- **"How long?":** "Less than 10 minutes. Maybe like 15 max."
- **Beginner (Johnny) version:** "I came across your website earlier and I noticed a few things that needs to be improved. So, I went ahead and made you a brand new website from scratch for completely free. It's already done. I just want to see if you have five to 10 minutes today or tomorrow"
- **Price question:** "What's the cost of that?" → "it really does depend … that's the thing we're going to be also talking about as soon as you see the website." The booking still happened.
- **No-show reducer (verbatim):** "Just a heads up. I've spent quite a bit of time working on this website, so I just want to confirm that you're sure you can make it at noon so we don't waste each other's time or anything."
- **Close:**
  - "Does it work before 12 or after 12?"
  - "What email would be the best to send you the Google Meet link to?"
- **Outcome:** 3 booked (1 same-day in 15 minutes, 2 for Tuesday).
- **Claimed:** 2–3 meetings a day.
- **Flags:**
  - "completely free" framing.
  - The "hosted on my laptop" excuse.

### #3 Kai Stone: "How i book 3-5 meetings a day Cold Calling ($319,000/month…)" (US) and script doc
- **What he sells:** He shows the "demo" is a GoHighLevel home-services template with the business name, phone and location swapped in: "I'm going to use the same template for literally every single person."
- **Opener (verbatim, DECEPTIVE):** "my friend Julie Becker actually uh my mom's friend kind of gave me your number. She said you guys do like really good work, but you guys don't have like a website or anything like that. Is that Is that true?" He later admits on camera that "Julie Becker" is a pattern-interrupt device: "You can argue if it's ethical or not." His suggested alternative is to name a real Google reviewer, which is still a false referral.
- **Second false element:** "I'm just like a web design student … it's kind of like part of like a school thing". He runs a $319k/month agency. **Avoid.**
- **Useful honest moves inside the call:**
  - Discovery question: "Why don't you have a website? What is there any specific reason?"
  - Search framing: "you type in like … barber near me, right? So, it'll be the same thing for plumbing services … And if you don't pop up, then you don't get the business"
  - Answers "What about Angie's?": "Angie's List is more like pay per lead … the website's basically organic. They basically come directly to you."
  - Meeting ask: "there's no pressure. I already have it built out for you … would you open to like getting on a Zoom call later today or tomorrow and I could just maybe show it to you? And if you like it, you can keep it. If not, you can tell me to uh to go play in trains."
  - Two-slot time offer: "I have 3:00 and 5:00 p.m."
  - Length: "15 minutes tops maybe, probably 10."
- **"Free ain't nothing free in this world" objection (verbatim answer):** "I get it. So, I will I will say there's a catch. So, if if I show it to you and you do like it … the whole website build is free, but we obviously would still have to charge you like hosting and stuff."
- **"No funny business?":** "No funny business, man. I promise you … I'm not trying to pull anything sketchy."
- **Philosophy (verbatim):** "The only thing you are trying to do with that cold call is to get them on the Zoom appointment … You're not trying to sell the product yet. You are selling the Zoom call"
- **Outcome:** 2 of 2 shown calls booked (plumber same day; handyman 4 p.m.). Both were confirmed by text, not email, at the prospect's request.
- **Script doc highlights (verbatim excerpts):**
  - "always use the cheapest foot in the door service you offer as the opener… we just want them to get on a call"
  - Time close: "Don't give them too many options… say 'How's "x time" today, I also have "x time open tomorrow", what works better for you?'"
  - Show-up check: "besides the off chance there's a zombie apocalypse at 'x appointment time' would there be any reason you wouldn't show up?"
  - Show-up incentive: "at the very least you can learn a little bit about your business from an advertising perspective + see what some of your competitors are doing to market themselves."
  - Objection "we already have enough business": "If you're already turning away business you can now quote the overflow you get at a higher price"
  - Objection "too expensive": ask what an average job is worth, then "if my service only gets them one job a year they would 4x, 5x, 10x their return"
  - Follow-up: "I have made the majority of my closes off calling people back between 5-11 times"
  - Post-call: send a case study immediately, then a thank-you text "so that the next time you text them they will more easily recognize who it is coming from"
- **Claimed by creator:**
  - $319k/month MRR.
  - 3–5 meetings/day cold calling 6–8 hours/day.
  - Setter KPIs: "5 hours = (~150 calls a day!)", "1-2 Booked appointments per day", "5-10 Booked appointments per week", "3-5 of these appointments should show up."

### #4 Pavlo: "Watch me book 3-5 meetings a day (web design cold calling)" (US)
- **Style:** He calls it "the buffoon technique", meaning direct "do you want this or not" questions.
- **Opener (verbatim):** "hey uh sorry you you broke up there for a second did you say this is BNB … I wasn't sure if uh you guys were open it it said closed for some reason I I didn't see a website or anything you guys are still like open and and doing service calls right"
- **Demo reveal (verbatim):** "I actually built you guys a a beautiful website if if I do say so myself uh if you like it you can you can keep it is that something you would you would even want to look at"
- **"Already built?":** "I have the homepage built uh I didn't build out the rest of the site I believe in giving value first so I could show you the homepage". This is an honest scope statement worth copying.
- **"Can you send it to me by email":** "well it's not live like it's not on it's not online … I have it here on my computer so if you want I could just share my screen and show you on Zoom"
- **"Already working with someone else":**
  - Asks: "do they already have it live for you or no"
  - Then: "do you even want to look at what I built for you cuz I can still show you"
  - Asks the competitor's price, then: "you can just choose whichever one you like best … does that sound fair"
- **Prospect already has a site he couldn't find:** offers value on the call: "I can show you how to get it back on Google … you don't have to pay me anything."
- **Outcome:** 4 meetings booked (3 p.m., 2:30 p.m., 3:30 p.m., 9 a.m. next day).
- **Claimed:** sells a "$150 … done in a day" low-ticket website; "called for a few hours".
- **Flags:**
  - "sorry you broke up there" is a fake bad-line pattern interrupt.
  - "it said closed for some reason" is a pretext used on nearly every call, regardless of what Google shows.
  - Automated SMS and voicemail on no-answer (shown in #5).

### #5 Pavlo: "How i book 3-5 meetings a day (selling websites cold calling)" (US, Utah)
- **Format:** Most connects are inbound callbacks from missed calls: "Hi, this is Gwen. I missed a call from you." → "Um, yeah. I think I called you guys like 20 minutes ago, right?"
- **Opener (verbatim):** "I actually built you guys a talking website. It's basically ready to book appointments and I could take a lot of these calls, too. Is that is I mean is that something you would even want to see?"
- **Objection "I'm kind of winding down… enough repeat customers":**
  - "it can also automatically book you these kinds of appointments … from your existing customers"
  - then "I mean, like I said, it's already done. I mean, do you want to just see it?"
- **"Send me a sample":** "it's already done … I don't have it live on the internet, so I can't just send you a link, but if you want, I can show it to you … we can do a Zoom call"
- **"Moving locations / word of mouth":**
  - "I could just show you what I have and at the very least you'll get some ideas … we can also look at like what's the search volume … in Wyoming"
  - then "Most of our clients … do word of mouth … I'm not charging you any money. I could just show you what I have."
- **Soft-book close for unsure prospects (verbatim):** "Let's just schedule something and then if we have to reschedule it, I'll call you the same day and we'll just pick a better time." He also says: "I'll just book you in for Friday, and then on Friday, I'll call you and see if that time is still good."
- **Outcome:** 3 meetings booked (Gwen Friday 8 p.m., Julio 6 p.m. same day, Randy Friday, Rulan Thursday 6 p.m.). That is 4 names; one booking is soft.
- **Flags:**
  - The "said closed when I tried to look you up" pretext again.
  - The GHL automation shown: "wait 1 minute … automatically text them … automatically leave them a voicemail … SMS 2, voicemail 2, a day later". These are automated texts to numbers that never opted in.

### #6 Pavlo: "Watch Me Cold Call LIVE & Get Rejected 😂 1 meeting booked :(" (US, Utah; livestream)
- **Session:** Uses the same opener as #4/#5. About 30 minutes in, 1 meeting booked. Many IVRs, voicemails and hang-ups.
- **Booked-out objection (Black Diamond / Crown Point):**
  - "How many months out are you booked?"
  - Observation: "I only saw six reviews"
  - Value nugget: hours showing "closed" after hours "might just call somebody else"
  - Soft book: "Monday at 5:00 p.m.… I'll shoot you a text like that same day"
- **Flags (do not copy):**
  - Leaves "Yo, call me back" voicemails with no name or reason, to provoke callbacks.
  - Tells an AI receptionist "need to talk with the owner. I have a complaint" to get escalated. **Clearly deceptive.**
  - Advises reading out specific negative reviews by reviewer name to gatekeepers ("Ken left you a one-star review").
  - The "said closed" pretext.
- **Practical note (verbatim):** "typically if you call them you really want 9:00 a.m. before they start their day."

### #7 Sean Cahill: "the best Cold Call you've ever seen (selling $1000+ AI websites like candy)" (US, Tampa)
- **Pre-call:** Searched "Tampa roofing companies" on Google Maps and noted businesses with no website. Called at about 7 p.m.
- **Opener (verbatim):** "Hey, how you doing? Is this A1 Roofing Services in Tampa Bay? … Are you the owner, by chance? … My name is um Shawn Cahill. I am a local website designer in the area, and just to keep it super quick with you. I don't want to waste your time or nothing. I I basically saw here on Google that you do not have a website. Is Is that correct?" → "Yeah, we're we're working on that."
- **Demo reveal:** "So, I actually went ahead and built you guys a website … went into the roofing industry, essentially, saw you guys, and went ahead and and built you out a a super clean website. And um I'd love the opportunity to show it to you"
- **Prospect probes:** "You already built me a website? … How long did that take you?" → "nearly half a day's of work". **Flag:** he builds the site *after* booking, on camera, in minutes.
- **Price asked directly** ("I actually got one of these calls and how much do you charge"):
  - "So the website would be around $750 for … that's a one-time fee but then we also offer services to manage the websites from a month-to-month basis which would essentially be around $100 to $200"
  - "I own the website after you create it, correct?" → "Yeah it's your website."
- **Close:** "Are you free around 6:00 p.m. tomorrow? Would that work for you right after the working day?" → Google Meet or Zoom with screen share.
- **Outcome:** Booked. The prospect said: "Everybody was highballing me with these crazy prices". **Relevance for Vanguard:** our $2,000 price will meet price-shoppers. Disclosing a range honestly did not kill this booking.

### #8 GHL Wizard: "Watch Me Book 5–7 Meetings a Day (AI Demo Cold Calling)" (US)
- **Opener (verbatim, DECEPTIVE):** "Hey, are you guys open right now? I'm looking online and it says you guys are closed." The prospect thinks he's a customer. The caller then pivots to "nobody is answering after hours … Is that happening to you guys as well?"
- **Demo reveal (verbatim):** "the reason I'm calling is because I basically built this AI agent that's going to handle sales and customer support 24/7. And to be clear, man, I'm not trying to sell you anything on this call … I'm just trying to give you a demo you can test." He then asks "either an email or a number that I can send this exact demo through". The prospect chooses text.
- **Demo instruction:** "go in, ask as if you're a customer … and try to break it."
- **Callback close:**
  - "I saw you looked at the site on the back end" (it tracks views).
  - Free setup offer: "I normally charge around $500 setup fee."
  - Then "Normally, I charge around like 300 bucks a month for this. I'll give it to you for 197."
- **Prospect objection (verbatim):** "is this like one of those things where you let me try it free, and then you charge me a bunch of money afterward?" Answer: "Not exactly…" followed by the $197/month reveal.
- **Outcome:** One booking/close shown, plus a rejection montage ("I'm not interested in whatever you're trying to sell me").
- **Flags:**
  - Posing as a customer.
  - "Not trying to sell you anything" while selling.
  - Anchoring on "normally $500 / $300" discounts, which may be fake urgency or fake anchors.
- **Idea worth keeping:** a demo link the prospect can open, plus a scheduled callback.

### #10 Danyyil Giba: "Watch Me FAIL Cold Calls Live (Selling Websites + AI Receptionist)" (Canada, Toronto painters)
- **Opener (verbatim):** "Hey Adam, this is Daniel here. You're going to hate me, but this is actually I'm here to offer you something that will make you money. Do you want to hear me out or hang up?"
- **Website variant:** "Hi. This is uh actually a sales call. Do you want to hang up or give me 30 seconds?" → "make it very quick. I'll give you a four" → "I'm going to redesign your website … I can show it to you for free". The prospect admits the site is bad but is booked until July and does it herself.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Mostly booked-out or happy owners ("17 years … word of mouth … I'm happy").
  - One owner asked for a callback the next day at noon.
  - No bookings.

- **Flags:**

  - Recommends SMS, WhatsApp and form-fill bombardment across multiple days.
  - "I'm calling because I just went to your website and it's not loading" is OK only if true.
  - Called at 6 p.m. and the prospect objected.

### #13 Max Ross: "I Cold Called 25 Local Businesses (web design cold calling)" (UK) — negative example
- **Opener (verbatim):** "I've actually got something I wanted to speak to you about if you have 30 seconds … my name is Max I'm a business owner like yourself and at the moment we're actually creating free website designs for local businesses in [area] with no strings attached … I did check out your current website and I know there quite a few things that could be improved to get you more inquiries … would you be opposed if we built one out for your business completely free of charge over the next few days and then … present it to you over a quick call"

  - "booked … until next March"
  - "I used to have the website I never got any more custom from that website"
  - "I'm not interested in any marketing or sales at the moment" (knee-jerk, before the pitch)
- **His diagnosis (verbatim):**
  - "I definitely could have made the tonality sound less like I was going to pitch him something"
  - "make it more outcome focused"

  - "I would want 100 pickups before I made any sort of conclusions"
- **Lesson for Vanguard:** "We're *going to build* you something" is weaker than "I *already built* it, here's what's on it". A vague "something I want to talk about" invites the knee-jerk no.

## 3. Patterns: the 10 most useful, repeatable moves for Vanguard's offer

These are filtered for honesty. Each move only works if the claim in it is true: the demo really exists, and we really looked at their Google profile.

1. **Confirm the business and the owner first, by name if possible.**
   - "Is this A1 Roofing Services in Tampa Bay? … Are you the owner, by chance?" (#7 Sean Cahill)
   - "Hey is this Victor? … Hey Victor this is Spencer" (#1 Spencer Reist)
   - Kai Stone's doc: "you can usually find their name in their google reviews" (#3)
   - Spencer's rule: never pitch the gatekeeper (#1).

2. **Give a truthful, specific "why you" from their Google profile: compliment, then the gap.**
   - "I noticed you have some great reviews but didn't have a website" (#1)
   - "I just found you guys on Google. Fantastic reviews, by the way. And I noticed you didn't have a website" (#2)
   - "I saw you had 21 five-star reviews, but you haven't had one in the last 8 months" (#15)
   - Real details (review count, rating, services) are what make the call sound researched rather than generic. Compare #13, where the generic opener got 0 meetings.

3. **Turn the gap into a yes/no question so they talk first.**
   - "I basically saw here on Google that you do not have a website. Is that correct?" (#7)
   - Kai's discovery question, minus the fake referral: "Why don't you have a website? Is there any specific reason?" (#3)
   - Their answer ("we're working on that", "word of mouth", "don't know how") tells you which objection script to use.

4. **Reveal the demo in one sentence, then ask a low-pressure "do you even want to see it?"**
   - "believe it or not I actually went ahead and built you a brand new beautiful website to help you get more jobs on Google" (#1)
   - "is that something you would even want to look at" (#4/#5 Pavlo)
   - Pavlo's honest scope line is worth copying when only part is built: "I have the homepage built … I didn't build out the rest" (#4).

5. **Sell the meeting, not the site, and time-box it.**
   - "10 to 15 minutes tomorrow to jump on a quick video call to check it out" (#1)
   - "15 minutes tops maybe, probably 10" (#3)
   - "a 15-minute chat … just to see if we're a good fit" (#9)
   - Kai: "You are selling the Zoom call" (#3). This matches the setter's only job.

6. **Risk-reversal line, adapted for a paid offer.**
   - Sourced lines:
     - "If you like it, we can publish it. If you don't, I'll go kick rocks." (#2)
     - "if you like it, you can keep it. If not, you can tell me to … go play in trains" (#3)
   - **Vanguard adaptation (ours, not sourced):** "If you like it, we'll talk about getting it live; if not, no hard feelings."
   - Do **not** say "free / keep it". Our build is $2,000 (see Avoid section).

7. **Use a two-slot time close, then lock the invite while still on the line.**
   - Two slots:
     - "How's X today, I also have X tomorrow, what works better for you?" (#3 doc)
     - "I have 3:00 and 5:00 p.m." (#3)
     - "Does it work before 12 or after 12?" (#2)
   - Lock it in:
     - "What email would be the best to send you the Google Meet link to?" (#2)
     - Send it during the call: "I like to make sure it goes to your email with you on the call" (#15)

8. **Get consent for reminders, and a commitment check to reduce no-shows.**
   - Reminder consent:
     - "is this a cell number? Can I just send you a link to that Zoom call here through a text?" (#9)
     - "do you get texts at this number … I'll remind you over text as well" (#1)
   - Commitment check:
     - "I've spent quite a bit of time working on this website, so I just want to confirm that you're sure you can make it at noon so we don't waste each other's time" (#2)
     - Kai doc: "would there be any reason you wouldn't show up?" plus a show-up incentive: "see what some of your competitors are doing" (#3)

9. **For unsure "call me later" prospects, soft-book and confirm the same day.**
   - "Let's just schedule something and then if we have to reschedule it, I'll call you the same day and we'll just pick a better time." (#5 Pavlo)
   - This beats "I'll call you back", which Kai's doc counters with: "Even better than a call back let me just schedule into my calendar." (#3)

10. **Answer price and "what's the catch" honestly and briefly, then go back to the meeting.**
    - Price: Sean stated "$750 … one-time … $100 to $200" a month and still booked; the prospect said others were "highballing" (#7). Darby gave "from £399 … half up front" and booked (#12).
    - Catch: Kai's "Free ain't nothing free" answer was "I will say there's a catch…" (#3).
    - ROI line (Kai doc): ask what one job is worth, then "if [it] only gets them one job a year they would 4x, 5x, 10x their return" (#3).
    - **For Vanguard:** a truthful line such as "Builds are usually around $2,000 one-time; the call is just to see if it's a fit."

**Honorable mentions (objection handling):**
- "Already have someone building it": "take some of those ideas … to your own guy" (#12), or "you can just choose whichever one you like best" (#4).
- "We're booked out / have enough work": don't argue; ask "how far out are you booked?" (#6, #10). Kai's overflow-pricing reframe also applies (#3 doc).
- "Not interested in a website": "most my clients say the exact same thing before they actually see the website" (#2).
- "I get work from word of mouth": "most of our clients … do word of mouth" (#5); "if you are growing off referrals … the first thing they will check out is that website" (#13).
- Pace: speak slowly and "unbothered", because rushing signals salesman (#14). Call around 9 a.m. before trades start their day (#6).
- Follow-up: "majority of my closes off calling people back between 5-11 times"; send a thank-you text right after the call "so … they will more easily recognize who it is coming from" (#3 doc). Only text where consent was given.

## 4. Doesn't work / avoid

**Deceptive or non-compliant tactics seen (do NOT use):**

1. **Fake referral.**
   - "my mom's friend Julie Becker … gave me your number" (Kai Stone #3). He concedes "You can argue if it's ethical or not."
   - His fallback of naming a real Google reviewer as the referrer is also a false referral.

2. **False persona.** "I'm just like a web design student … part of like a school thing" from an owner claiming $319k/month (#3).

3. **Posing as a customer, or using a pretext about their listing.**
   - "Are you guys open right now? I'm looking online and it says you guys are closed" (GHL Wizard #8).
   - "it said closed for some reason", used as a stock opener on nearly every call (Pavlo #4/#5/#6).

4. **Fake bad-line pattern interrupt.** "sorry you broke up there", used before the prospect has said anything meaningful (Pavlo #4/#5/#6; Payton #9).

5. **Claiming the site is built when it isn't.**
   - Goutham admits "he thought I was lying, which I was" (#11).
   - Sean says "nearly half a day's of work" and then builds it after the booking (#7).
   - Vanguard's demo must genuinely exist before the dial, which matches our process.

6. **Unverifiable monitoring claims.** "We get notified anytime like a site gets broken or hacked" (Payton #9).

7. **"I'm not trying to sell you anything" followed by a pitch** (#8, #11). The "normally $500 setup / $300 a month, I'll give it to you for 197" anchor may be fake (#8).

8. **Excuses for not sending a link** that may be untrue: "hosted on my laptop" (#2), "I can't publish a website" (#5). If a preview link can be shared, don't claim otherwise. Give a true reason for wanting the call instead.

9. **Lying to gatekeepers or AI receptionists.** "I have a complaint" to get escalated to the owner (Pavlo #6).

10. **Context-free voicemails meant to bait a callback.** "Yo, call me back" (#6).

11. **Unconsented messaging.**
    - Automated "text them … leave them a voicemail … SMS 2, voicemail 2" workflows on no-answer (Pavlo #5/#4).
    - Looking up numbers on WhatsApp/iMessage and messaging without opt-in (Danyyil #10).
    - In the US, automated or marketing texts to cell numbers raise TCPA / A2P 10DLC consent issues. Have this checked by counsel; it is not legal advice.

12. **Reading out a business's negative reviews by reviewer name to staff** as a "hack" (#6). It's off-putting and borders on pressure.

**Things that underperformed or don't fit our offer:**
- **"Free website, keep it if you like it."** Most creators lead with "free". Vanguard charges about $2,000 upfront, so a "free" opener sets up a bait-and-switch on the closer's call. GHL Wizard's prospect said it outright: "is this like one of those things where you let me try it free, and then you charge me a bunch of money afterward?" (#8). Use "already built, no obligation to look", not "free".
- **Vague, future-tense openers.** "I've got something I wanted to speak to you about if you have 30 seconds … we're creating free website designs" got 0 meetings from about 12 pickups. The creator blamed pitchy tonality and a lack of outcome focus (#13).
- **"You're going to hate me / do you want to hang up?" openers.** These earn a few seconds but led to quick "I'm good" exits in #10 (0 bookings). They worked once in #15, where a specific review observation followed immediately. If used at all, follow with a concrete, true detail at once.
- **Pitching extra products on the first call** (AI receptionist, missed-call text-back, reviews) to owners who are already booked. All the Toronto painters in #10 declined. Keep the setter on one thing: see the demo.
- **Pushing busy or booked-out owners.** Every creator who pushed past "booked till March" or "I'm happy, 17 years, word of mouth" got nothing (#10, #13). Log it and move on, or soft-book a later date.
- **Calling at dinnertime.** "You're calling me at like 6:00 for a business thing" (#10). Early morning before jobs is the suggested window (#6).
- **Judging a script on too little data.** Max Ross: wait for about 100 *pickups*, not dials, before changing it (#13).
