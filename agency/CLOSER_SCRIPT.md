# Vanguard — Owner Consultation & Close Script

**Role:** Owner / Closer
**Call type:** 15–30 minute consultation (booked by setter)
**Goal:** Understand their problem, show the demo, present the offer, close or get a clear next step
**Framework:** StoryBrand (you are the guide; they are the hero) + SPIN Selling discovery

---

## Pre-Call Prep Checklist

Do this before every call:

- [ ] Review setter notes in CRM — what objections came up? What resonated?
- [ ] Pull up their current website and spend 2 minutes on it
- [ ] Pull up the demo site — make sure it loads and looks right
- [ ] Know their niche and which social proof client fits best (Christ United / Elixa Beauty / MagniFit / Vanguard Structures)
- [ ] Have your offer and pricing ready (don't wing the numbers)
- [ ] Clear your environment — no distractions, good audio, camera on if video call
- [ ] Set a 30-minute timer — respect their time and yours

---

## Opening — Build Rapport, Set the Agenda

**Goal:** Make them feel comfortable and in control. Set the frame for the call.

> "Hey [First Name], thanks for jumping on. I'm [Your Name] — I'm the owner over at Vanguard. I really appreciate you taking the time."

*(30 seconds of genuine small talk — ask something about their business or location that shows you did homework)*

> "Before we dive in — I want to make sure this call is actually useful for you. Here's what I was thinking: I'll ask you a few questions about what's going on with your business and your website right now. Then I'll pull up the demo we built and walk you through it. After that, if it makes sense, I can talk about how we work and what it might look like to do this together. And if it doesn't make sense, I'll tell you that too. Does that sound good?"

**Why this works:** You're framing yourself as an advisor, not a salesperson. You're also getting them to say yes early.

---

## SPIN Discovery Questions

Take real notes. This is where you earn the close. Go slow. Don't rush to the demo.

**Transition line:**

> "To make sure the demo is actually relevant to you, let me ask a few quick questions — I promise I won't keep you all day."

---

### Situation Questions
*(Keep these to a minimum — anything you could find on their site, Google profile, or the setter's notes, look up before the call instead of asking.)*

> "How do most of your new customers find you right now — word of mouth, Google, social media, something else?"

> "Are you running any ads, or is it mostly organic at this point?"

> "How's business been lately — are you happy with where things are, or are you trying to grow?"

---

### Problem Questions
*(Surface the pain — what's not working)*

> "When someone does find you online, what do you think their first impression is of the site?"

> "Have you ever had someone mention your website in a negative way — or had a lead go cold and you suspected the site might have played a role?"

> "Is there anything on the site right now that you know is outdated or just doesn't reflect the business well?"

> "If someone lands on your page right now from Google at 9pm — can they book, contact you, or find what they need without you being involved?"

> "On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that your website is actually helping you get clients — not just existing?"

---

### Implication Questions
*(Help them feel the cost of the problem — connect it to revenue and stress)*

> "So if the site isn't converting or isn't up to date — what does that look like for you? Are you having to work harder to get referrals? Spending more on ads?"

> "If someone Googled you right now and landed on your site compared to a competitor — what do you think they'd do?"

> "You mentioned most business comes from word of mouth. What happens if that slows down — do you have a way to generate new leads online?"

> "Has there been a time where you felt like you lost a job or a client because the online presence just didn't match the quality of work you actually do?"

*(Listen carefully here. The implication answers are your close material — they'll tell you exactly what to say later.)*

---

### Need-Payoff Questions
*(Let them articulate what a solution is worth — they sell themselves)*

> "If your website was working the way it should — someone lands on it, immediately trusts you, and books a call or fills out a form — what would that mean for your business?"

> "If you were getting even 2–3 more qualified leads per month from your site, what does that look like in revenue?"

> "What would it mean for you personally if you didn't have to worry about whether your online presence was costing you clients?"

> "If your site looked as good as your actual work — how do you think that would change the way clients perceive you before they even talk to you?"

---

## Walking Through the Demo

**Transition:**

> "Okay — I want to show you what we built. Give me one second to pull it up."

*(Share screen or send link)*

> "So this is what we put together for [Business Name]. We started with your current site and thought about what someone who found you online would actually need to see to feel confident reaching out."

**Walk the demo in this order:**

1. **Hero section** — "This is the first thing people see. Clear message, clear call to action. Right now your site makes people work to figure out what you do. This just says it."
2. **Services / offer section** — "We listed out your core services the way a customer would think about them — not industry jargon."
3. **Social proof / trust section** — "This is where trust gets built. Reviews, photos, results — whatever makes someone feel like you're the safe choice."
4. **Contact / booking** — "And this is the money section. A form, a button, a phone number — however someone wants to reach you, it's right here. No hunting."

**After walking it:**

> "What's your honest reaction? What do you like, what would you change?"

*(Listen. Don't defend. Take notes. This tells you what they want.)*

---

## Presenting the Offer

**Only present after discovery and demo — never before.**

**First, check concerns and summarize (SPIN):** before any price, make sure nothing is left open and replay *their* words back to them.

> "Before I walk you through how this works — is there anything about the demo or what we talked about that you'd want me to go deeper on?"

> "So let me make sure I've got it. You said [problem in their words], and that it's costing you [implication in their words]. And you said what you really want is [need-payoff answer in their words]. Did I get that right?"

*(Wait for a yes. That yes is an Explicit Need — now the offer is a Benefit, not a pitch.)*

**Present the offer** (full details in OFFER.md):

> "What I'd put together for you is what we call the 21-Day Authority Website System. Custom design and build — not a template — plus we write all the copy for you off one kick-off call, set up your local SEO foundation, and give you two rounds of revisions and a handoff session so you're never stuck. And you're live in 21 days: if we miss that and you've given us feedback on time, your first month of care is on us."

Pick the tier that fits what they told you — recommend one, don't read a menu:

| Tier | Price | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| **The Foundation** | $1,800–$2,200 | 5 pages · solo providers, small churches, beauty/wellness solos |
| **The Full Presence** | $2,400–$2,800 | 7–8 pages + booking, gallery, testimonials · gyms, contractors, multi-service |
| **Care Plan** (add-on) | $150–$250/mo, cancel anytime | Hosting, security, backups, 1 hr/month of updates |

> "For what you described, I'd recommend [tier] — that's [price]. That's paid up front when you sign, and we start right away. After launch, most clients add the Care Plan so they never have to think about the site again."

**Then stop talking. Let them respond.**

## Handling the Top 5 Objections

---

### 1. "The price is too high / I can't afford that right now."

> "I hear you — it's not a small number. Can I ask — what part feels off? Is it that the budget genuinely isn't there, or is it more that you're not sure it's worth it yet?"

*(Listen)*

If budget is the issue:
> "We can start with The Foundation — five core pages at $1,800–$2,200 — and add booking and a gallery later when it makes sense. Same 21-day launch, same guarantee. Would that work better?"

If value is the issue:
> "Fair. Let me ask you this: you mentioned [implication answer — e.g., 'losing clients to competitors with better-looking sites']. What does one new client from your website represent in revenue to you?"

*(Let them do the math)*

> "So if the site brings in even one new client a month — it pays for itself in [X months]. That's kind of the lens I'd put on it."

---

### 2. "The timing isn't right — maybe in a few months."

> "Totally understand. What's going on between now and then?"

*(Listen — is it real, or is it a stall?)*

> "The reason I'd push back a little is that your website is either working for you or against you right now. Waiting doesn't pause the problem — it just means [competitor, who already has a better site] gets those leads in the meantime. Is there a specific thing that needs to happen first, or is it more of a cash flow thing?"

If timing is genuine:
> "That's fair. What I'd suggest is this — let me put together a formal proposal so you have the numbers in front of you when you're ready. And let's put a date on the calendar for when you want to revisit it. Does [specific date] work?"

---

### 3. "I need to think about it."

Say this without any edge — be genuinely curious:

> "Of course. Can I ask what part you're still thinking through?"

*(Let them answer)*

> "That makes sense. I'm not trying to pressure you — I just want to make sure you have what you need to make a good call. Is there something I didn't explain well, or a piece of information that would help?"

If they're stalling:
> "Honestly — in my experience, when someone needs to think about it, it usually means one of two things: either the price feels off, or they're not fully convinced it'll work. Which one is it for you? I'd rather you just tell me."

---

### 4. "My nephew / friend / someone I know does websites."

Don't compete. Differentiate.

> "That's great — do they do this professionally, or is it more of a side thing?"

*(Listen)*

> "Here's the honest answer: if they're good and you trust them, that might actually be the right call. What I'd ask is — have you seen the work they do for businesses like yours? And is this something they can turn around in [your timeline] without it creating an awkward situation?"

> "The reason people come to us is that we specialize in this specifically — service businesses, local markets — and the whole project runs through me. There's no handing off to someone else, no delays waiting on a freelancer. But if your nephew can do what I just showed you in the demo, that's a real option."

*(Most people don't say yes after this — they start to see the gap.)*

---

### 5. "Can you just send me a proposal?"

> "Absolutely — I'll get that over to you today. Before I do — is there anything in our conversation that felt unclear, or anything that's making you want to step back and look at it on paper?"

*(Listen for the real objection)*

> "Got it. I'll put together a clean proposal with the scope, price, and timeline. I'll send it by end of day. One favor — can we set a time now to talk through it? Even 15 minutes. In my experience, proposals that don't get a follow-up call just sit in inboxes. What does [2 days from now] look like for you?"

**Always get a follow-up call scheduled before you send the proposal.**

---

## The Close — Exact Words

After presenting the offer and handling objections, if there's a clear yes signal:

> "So — do you want to move forward?"

*(Simple. Direct. Let the silence sit.)*

Or if you want to frame the next step:

> "Here's what I'd suggest: you sign and pay today, I'll send you the onboarding form tomorrow, and your new site is live within 21 days. Does that work?"

If they say yes:
> "Perfect. I'll send the invoice and agreement over in the next hour. Once that's in, we're off."

---

## Next Steps / Handoff

**If they close on the call:**
- Send invoice within 1 hour
- Send onboarding form / kick-off questionnaire same day
- Log in CRM as Closed Won
- Begin intake

**If they want a proposal:**
- Send within 24 hours
- Book follow-up call before you hang up
- Log outcome and follow-up date in CRM

**If they're not ready:**
- Log reason in CRM
- Agree on a re-contact date ("Can I check back in on [date]?")
- Pass back to setter for re-nurture cadence if 30+ days out

---

## Mindset Notes

**You are the guide. They are the hero.**
Your job isn't to convince them — it's to help them see clearly what's already true: their website is costing them, and you know how to fix it. You've done it for Christ United, Elixa Beauty, MagniFit, and Vanguard Structures. You're not guessing.

**The silence after the close is your friend.** Most people talk themselves into a yes if you let them. Don't fill it.

**Confidence is not pushiness.** A confident expert tells you the truth and lets you decide. A pushy salesperson manipulates. Be the expert.

**Honest urgency only.** Scarcity ("I have two build slots this month") is only allowed when it's true. Skip any tactic that invents a reason — e.g. the "ID close" in the `hormozi-sales` skill claims ID is needed "for PCI compliance," which it isn't.

**If it's not a fit, say so.** Chasing bad-fit clients costs more than passing on them. The fastest way to build trust is to tell someone "I don't think this is right for you" when it's true.
