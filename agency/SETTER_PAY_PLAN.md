# Vanguard — Setter Compensation Plan

**Role:** Setter (cold calls local businesses, books consultations with the owner)
**Status:** Draft v1 for owner review. Rates below are recommendations, not settled terms. See Section 7 before anyone signs anything.
**Related docs:** PIPELINE.md (stages, targets), OFFER.md (pricing, payment terms), SETTER_SCRIPT.md (calling and texting rules)

---

## TL;DR for the owner

- **Recommended:** **$30 per qualified show + 8% of collected project cash** on deals that come from the setter's appointments, plus two small monthly bonuses.
- **At target metrics** (3 booked/day, 70% show, 30% close, $2,300 avg deal) a setter earns about **$895/week, ~$4,170/month** including bonus.
- **Cost to Vanguard:** about **$284 per closed deal before bonuses (12.3% of $2,300)**, about **$306 (13.3%) with bonuses**. It stays under 15% unless close rate drops below 20%.
- **Nothing is paid** on dials, bookings, no-shows, signed-but-unpaid contracts, or any lead that broke the DNC or texting rules.
- **Before launch:** have a lawyer/accountant confirm worker classification, minimum-wage exposure and the written-agreement rules for the setter's state (Section 7).

## 1. Structure: recommended plan plus two alternatives

### Recommended: Show fee + commission on collected cash (hybrid)

| Component | Amount | Paid when |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified show fee | **$30** per qualified show (Section 3) | Next payout after the owner marks it qualified |
| Commission | **8% of cash actually collected** on the website project (deposit and launch payment) | Next payout after each payment clears |
| Monthly bonuses | Small, capped (Section 4) | First payout of the following month |

What's excluded from commission (to keep it simple):
- **Care Plan revenue.** The owner sells and services it, and the setter doesn't influence it. Revisit later if you want setters to push it.
- **Sales tax and refunded amounts**, if any.
- **Cash collected more than 120 days after the qualified show.** Covers normal delays to the launch payment without leaving commission open forever.

**Why this is the recommendation for a solo-owner shop at ~$2,300 deals:**
- The show fee pays the setter for the part they control: getting a real decision-maker onto the call. That lowers the pressure to "sell" on the phone, which SETTER_SCRIPT.md says not to do.
- The commission ties most of the pay to cash in the bank, so a setter gains nothing by booking people who will never buy.
- Cost per deal is self-limiting: a bad week costs you a few $30 show fees, not a salary.
- It works under either a contractor or an employee arrangement (though the minimum-wage point in Section 7 still applies to employees).

**Cons:**
- Income is lumpy for the setter, especially in month 1 while they ramp and the launch payments haven't arrived yet.
- Some of the setter's pay depends on the owner's closing, which the setter doesn't control. The show fee softens this but doesn't remove it.
- It takes GHL discipline: the show and payment fields have to be kept up to date (Section 6).

### Alternative A: Pay-per-qualified-show only

- **$75 per qualified show**, no commission.
- **Pros:** the simplest to run, paid fast, fully in the setter's control, no dispute over whether the owner "should have closed it."
- **Cons:** the owner carries all of the close-rate risk. At a 20% close rate you pay $375 per deal (16.3%). There's also more incentive to push lukewarm prospects onto the calendar, so the qualification rules have to be strictly enforced.
- **Best if:** close rate is stable at 40%+ and you want the lowest admin load.

### Alternative B: Hourly base + smaller commission (W-2 employee)

- **Hourly pay at or above the applicable minimum wage** (example: $15/hr × 20 hrs/week = $300/week) **+ 5% of collected project cash**.
- **Pros:** attracts steadier people, the setter can predict their income, the minimum-wage question is answered up front, and a W-2 employee on your schedule and scripts is the more conventional setup.
- **Cons:** a fixed cost whether or not anything closes (a ramp week with 0.75 closes costs about $515 per deal, 22%), plus payroll taxes, workers' comp, a payroll provider and more admin for a solo owner.
- **Best if:** you want one long-term setter, have steady cash flow and are ready to run payroll.

**Why not commission-only (% of cash, no show fee)?** It's cheapest on paper, but setters wait 3+ weeks for any pay, turnover is high, and if the setter is an employee it creates the biggest minimum-wage exposure (Section 7).

### Side-by-side (per closed deal, $2,300 avg deal, before bonuses and payroll taxes)

| Close rate | Recommended ($30/show + 8%) | Alt A ($75/show) | Alt B ($300/wk base + 5%)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | $334 (14.5%) | $375 (16.3%) | depends on volume |
| 30% | $284 (12.3%) | $250 (10.9%) | ~$210 (9.1%) at 3.15 closes/wk |
| 40% | $259 (11.3%) | $188 (8.2%) | lower as volume rises |
| 50% | $244 (10.6%) | $150 (6.5%) | lower as volume rises |

*Alt B's per-deal cost depends mostly on how many deals the fixed base is spread across. At the ramp scenario below (0.75 closes/week) it's ~$515/deal (22%).

## 2. Worked math (recommended plan)

### Inputs (from PIPELINE.md and OFFER.md)

- Booked meetings: 2–4/day → planning case **3/day, 5 days/week = 15/week**
- Show rate target: **70%**
- Close rate target: 30–50% → planning case uses the conservative **30%**
- Average deal: **$2,300** (between Foundation $1,800–$2,200 and Full Presence $2,400–$2,800)
- Payment terms: 50% deposit at kick-off, 50% at launch
- Weeks per month: 4.33

### Setter earnings: target case (3 booked/day, 70% show, 30% close)

```
Booked/week        = 3 × 5                 = 15
Shows/week         = 15 × 0.70             = 10.5
Closes/week        = 10.5 × 0.30           = 3.15

Show fees/week     = 10.5 × $30            = $315.00
Commission/deal    = $2,300 × 8%           = $184.00
Commission/week    = 3.15 × $184           = $579.60
Total/week                                 = $894.60

Monthly (× 4.33)   = $894.60 × 4.33        = $3,873.62
Shows/month        = 10.5 × 4.33           = 45.5  → hits the 40-show tier bonus (+$300)
Show-rate bonus    = 70% < 75%             = $0
Total/month                                ≈ $4,174
```

**Cash timing:** the commission is paid in two halves: $92 when the deposit clears and $92 when the launch payment clears (21+ days later). In the setter's first month they'll only see about the deposit half, so month 1 pays noticeably less than the steady-state figure above.

### Other scenarios

| Scenario | Booked/wk | Shows/wk | Closes/wk | Weekly pay | Monthly pay (incl. bonus) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ramp (1/day, 60% show, 25% close) | 5 | 3.0 | 0.75 | $228 | ~$987 |
| Low target (2/day, 70%, 30%) | 10 | 7.0 | 2.1 | $596 | ~$2,732 |
| **Target (3/day, 70%, 30%)** | **15** | **10.5** | **3.15** | **$895** | **~$4,174** |
| Target, better close (3/day, 70%, 40%) | 15 | 10.5 | 4.2 | $1,088 | ~$5,010 |
| High (4/day, 70%, 50%) | 20 | 14.0 | 7.0 | $1,708 | ~$7,696 |

The ramp row matters for Section 7: $228 for a full week of dialing may fall below minimum wage if the setter is an employee.

### Cost to Vanguard per closed deal

```
Show fees per close  = $30 ÷ close rate
At 30% close         = $30 ÷ 0.30 = $100   (3.33 shows per close)
Commission per close = $184
Setter cost / close  = $100 + $184 = $284
As % of $2,300       = $284 ÷ $2,300 = 12.3%

With bonuses (target case, monthly):
  Total monthly pay  = $4,174
  Closes/month       = 3.15 × 4.33 = 13.64
  Cost per close     = $4,174 ÷ 13.64 ≈ $306  → 13.3% of $2,300
```

| Close rate | Show fees per close | + Commission | Cost per close | % of $2,300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | $300 | $184 | $484 | 21.0% |
| 20% | $150 | $184 | $334 | 14.5% |
| 25% | $120 | $184 | $304 | 13.2% |
| 30% | $100 | $184 | $284 | 12.3% |
| 40% | $75 | $184 | $259 | 11.3% |
| 50% | $60 | $184 | $244 | 10.6% |

**What Vanguard keeps:** at the target case, about $1,994–$2,016 of each $2,300 deal is left before delivery costs (owner time, hosting, stock, tools, payment processing). The setter is roughly one-seventh of the revenue.

**Guardrails:**
- If the monthly close rate drops **below 20%**, setter cost goes above ~15% per deal. Treat that as a signal to check qualification (are the decision-makers real, are the prospects in a target niche) or the close, not to cut setter pay first. The hormozi-sales skill makes the same point: if offers are made on fewer than 80% of calls, qualification is failing.
- **Capacity check (owner to confirm):** the target case produces about **13–14 closed projects/month from one setter**, and more at higher close rates. Every one carries the 21-Day Launch Guarantee. Confirm you can deliver that volume before hiring a second setter or paying for volume above what you can build. If you can't, cap weekly bookings instead of lowering rates.

## 3. What counts as a paid appointment ("Qualified Show")

A booked call pays the $30 show fee only if **every** item below is true. The owner confirms this in GHL within 2 business days of the call.

**The lead**
1. **Assigned to the setter in GHL** before the first dial (Setter Assigned field).
2. **DNC-clean before the first dial:** scrubbed against the National Do Not Call Registry and the internal do-not-call list, with the scrub date logged. The scrub should be recent (the federal rule is commonly described as a 31-day refresh; see Section 7).
3. **Fits the target:** a target niche or approved "other," with a website rated None / Terrible / Outdated.
4. **Not a duplicate:** no other open opportunity for the same business, and not an existing client or a lead already in Proposal Sent. A business can earn one show fee per 90 days. If two setters touch a lead, the first logged booking owns it.

**How it was booked**
5. **The demo existed before the first dial:** the Demo Link and Demo Live Date are logged and dated before the first call attempt. "We already built it" has to be true.
6. **All calls were placed between 8am and 9pm in the prospect's time zone.**
7. **Texts only with consent:** every text to the lead happened after SMS consent (Y + date/time + how given) was logged. No consent means no texts, including reminders.
8. **No misrepresentation:** the prospect knew it was a call with Vanguard about their website. No promised pricing, free sites or results (SETTER_SCRIPT.md rules 1 and 5).
9. **Every touch logged in GHL the same day.**

**The call**
10. **The decision-maker attended:** the person who can approve and pay for the project. If a co-owner or board has to decide and wasn't there, it isn't qualified unless the owner chooses to count it.
11. **They showed:** the call happened and the owner got through the demo walkthrough. A no-show that the setter rebooks counts once, when they actually show.

**Commission rules**
- Commission is paid on **collected cash, not signed contracts or verbal yeses**. A payment counts once it has cleared the processor.
- **Clawback:** if a payment is refunded or charged back **within 30 days** of collection, the commission on that payment is deducted from the next payout. Refunds after 30 days don't claw back. Whether deductions like this are allowed where the setter works is a Section 7 item.
- Commission can be earned only on a deal that came from a Qualified Show. If that show is later disqualified for a compliance reason, the commission goes too (Section 5).

## 4. Bonuses and leaderboard (kept simple)

| Bonus | Rule | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Show tier | Qualified shows in a calendar month: **25+** / **40+** | **$150** / **$300** (not cumulative) |
| Show-rate kicker | Monthly show rate **≥ 75%** on at least 15 bookings | **$100** |
| Setter of the month | Only once there are 2+ setters: most qualified shows, with zero compliance flags | **$100** |

- Bonuses count **qualified shows only**. Dials and raw bookings earn nothing, so there's no reason to pad volume.
- A setter with **any** compliance flag that month (Section 5) isn't eligible for that month's bonuses.

**Discord #leaderboard**
- Posted **every Monday** to go with the weekly review in PIPELINE.md.
- It shows **qualified shows, show rate and closed-won deals** per setter for the week and month to date. **It never shows dollars or pay**; pay details go by private DM only.
- Dials can be listed for context, but **ranking is by qualified shows**. A lead with a compliance flag drops off the board.
- Keep it factual: numbers and a shout-out, no pressure posts.

## 5. Compliance-linked rules (the incentive never rewards cutting corners)

**Forfeiture:** any lead where the rules below were broken earns **$0**: no show fee, no commission and no bonus credit, even if it closes and pays.

| Rule broken | Result |
|---|---|
| Dialed without a logged DNC scrub, or dialed a number on the National DNC Registry or internal do-not-call list | Forfeit all comp on that lead |
| Texted without logged SMS consent, or kept texting after "STOP" | Forfeit all comp on that lead |
| Called outside 8am–9pm in the prospect's time zone | Forfeit all comp on that lead |
| Called again after "don't call me" (not added to the internal DNC list the same day) | Forfeit all comp on that lead |
| Demo didn't exist before the first dial, or the setter claimed facts that weren't true | Forfeit all comp on that lead |
| Fake or duplicate booking, or a non-decision-maker logged as one | Forfeit and review |

**How it's enforced**
- **Weekly spot-check:** the owner picks 3 random Qualified Shows per setter and checks the scrub date, consent record (if any texts were sent), demo date and call times in GHL.
- **Found later:** if a violation turns up after payment, comp on that lead is deducted from future payouts, subject to Section 7 confirmation on deductions.
- **Self-reporting:** a setter who reports their own mistake the same day loses comp on that lead only, with no further consequence. The point is to surface problems, not hide them.
- **Repeat or deliberate violations** end the arrangement.
- **Not paid for:** dials, texts, voicemails, emails or DMs. There's nothing to gain by skipping the scrub to dial faster.

## 6. Payout schedule and GHL tracking

### Schedule
- **Pay period:** two weeks, Monday to Sunday.
- **Payday:** the Friday after the period closes.
- **Show fees:** for shows marked Qualified by the Wednesday before payday.
- **Commission:** on project payments that cleared by the Wednesday before payday.
- **Bonuses:** paid on the first payday of the following month.
- **Clawbacks:** deducted on the next payday after a refund or chargeback within 30 days, if allowed (Section 7).
- **Statement:** each payday the setter gets a private Discord DM with an itemized statement listing each show fee and commission line by GHL contact name.
- Some states set minimum pay frequency for employees, so confirm biweekly works (Section 7).

### GHL: what proves a payable appointment

GHL pipeline stages should mirror PIPELINE.md: Cold Lead → Demo Built → Contacted → Meeting Booked → On Call → Proposal Sent → Closed Won / Closed Lost.

**Suggested custom fields** (names are suggestions; add the ones that don't already exist):

| Field | Type | Set by | Used to prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setter Assigned | User | Owner | Who owns the lead |
| DNC Scrub Date | Date | Setter | Rule 2 |
| Internal DNC | Checkbox | Setter | Rule 2 and Section 5 |
| SMS Consent / Consent Date-Time / Consent Method | Y/N, Date, Dropdown | Setter | Rule 7 |
| Demo Link / Demo Live Date | URL, Date | Setter / builder | Rule 5 |
| First Dial Date | Date | Setter (or from call log) | Rules 5–6 |
| Decision-Maker Present | Y/N | Owner | Rule 10 |
| **Qualified Show** | Y/N | **Owner only** | The show fee |
| Disqualify Reason | Dropdown | Owner | Audit trail |
| Deposit Collected ($ / date) | Number, Date | Owner | Commission |
| Launch Payment Collected ($ / date) | Number, Date | Owner | Commission |
| Refund / Chargeback ($ / date) | Number, Date | Owner | Clawback |
| Compliance Flag + note | Checkbox, Text | Owner | Forfeiture |

**A show is payable when all of these are true:**
1. The GHL calendar appointment status is **Showed**.
2. The opportunity is at **On Call or later**.
3. **Qualified Show = Yes** (only the owner can set it).
4. DNC Scrub Date and Demo Live Date are both **earlier than** First Dial Date.
5. Compliance Flag is **not** set.

**A commission is payable when** that same opportunity has a Deposit or Launch Payment Collected entry dated within the pay period, within 120 days of the show, and no refund logged. If payments go through GHL invoices, use the paid status. Otherwise the owner copies the amount from the payment processor.

**Payout routine (about 15 minutes each payday):**
1. In GHL, filter a Smart List: Qualified Show = Yes, show date in period, grouped by Setter Assigned.
2. Filter a second list for Deposit/Launch Payment Collected dates in the period.
3. Check refunds and flags, send each setter their statement, then pay.

## 7. Legal and admin to confirm (for a lawyer and accountant)

These are questions to take to a professional, not conclusions. The answers depend on the setter's state or country and on how the work is actually run.

1. **Contractor (1099) or employee (W-2)?** Vanguard supplies scripts, a required follow-up cadence, CRM rules, call-time rules, weekly reviews and spot-checks. Regulators often treat that kind of control as a sign of employment. The IRS, the U.S. Department of Labor and states each use different tests, and some states (for example, those using an "ABC" test such as California) are stricter. Ask which classification fits and what changes if a setter is outside the U.S.
2. **Minimum wage and overtime for commission-heavy employees.** If the setter is an employee, ask whether show fees plus commission must at least equal minimum wage for all hours worked **each workweek**, and how overtime applies. The ramp scenario ($228 for a full week) shows where this could bite. Ask whether any sales exemption could apply to inside phone setters, or whether a draw or hourly floor is needed.
3. **Written commission agreement.** Some states require commission plans to be in writing and signed, including California and New York. Ask what this plan needs to include for the setter's state: how commissions are calculated, when they're earned and paid, and what happens to unpaid commission when the setter leaves.
4. **Clawbacks and deductions.** Ask whether deducting refunded-deal commission or forfeited comp from future pay is allowed, and how the agreement must word "earned" to support it.
5. **Pay frequency and final pay.** Ask whether biweekly pay is allowed for commissioned employees, and when final pay (including commission on deals that pay after the setter leaves) is due.
6. **Telemarketing compliance for the calling itself.** Ask about the TCPA and Telemarketing Sales Rule as they apply to calls and texts to businesses, how often the National DNC Registry must be scrubbed, state "mini-TCPA" laws and any state telemarketer registration requirements, and whether call-recording consent is needed if calls are recorded. SETTER_SCRIPT.md already says to get a lawyer's sign-off before scaling texting.
7. **Tax paperwork.** For contractors: W-9 collection and 1099 reporting thresholds (confirm the current amount). For employees: payroll setup, withholding, unemployment insurance and workers' comp.

## 8. Setter one-pager (2-minute read)

**How you get paid at Vanguard**

**Your job:** get a real decision-maker onto a call with the owner to see the demo we already built for them. The owner handles pricing and closing.

**You earn:**
- **$30** for every **qualified show**, meaning the business owner or decision-maker actually joins the call and sees the demo.
- **8%** of the money the client actually pays for their website. On a typical $2,300 project that's **$184**: $92 when they pay the deposit and $92 when they pay at launch.
- **Monthly bonuses:** $150 at 25 qualified shows or $300 at 40. Another $100 if your show rate is 75% or better on 15+ bookings.

**What that can look like:** at 3 bookings a day, a 70% show rate and a 30% close rate, you'd earn about **$895 a week**, roughly **$4,000+ a month** once launch payments start arriving. Your first few weeks will be lower while you ramp and before second payments come in.

**What doesn't pay:** dials, texts, bookings that don't show, someone who isn't the decision-maker, a duplicate of someone else's lead, or a deal that was signed but never paid. If a client gets a refund within 30 days, that commission comes back out.

**What gets you $0 on a lead, even if it closes:**
- Calling before the number was DNC-scrubbed and logged
- Texting anyone who hasn't said yes to texts on a call, or ignoring STOP
- Calling outside 8am–9pm their time
- Calling again after they said "don't call me"
- Dialing before the demo was live, or saying anything that isn't true

If you make a mistake, tell the owner the same day. You'll lose pay on that lead and nothing more.

**How it's tracked:** everything lives in GHL. Log every touch the same day, fill in the scrub date, consent and demo fields, and the owner marks shows as qualified. **If it's not in GHL, it didn't happen.**

**When you're paid:** every other Friday, with an itemized statement sent to you privately on Discord. #leaderboard goes up every Monday and ranks qualified shows, never pay.

**Questions about a specific lead or payout:** DM the owner with the lead's GHL name.
