(01) Purpose

One job. Book it live.

Your one job

A confirmed call with the closer, booked while you're on the phone

Not "I'll send you a link." Not "call me back." A real time, in their calendar, with an invite they've already seen, before you hang up.

Everything on this call serves that: the opener earns 30 seconds, discovery earns the right to ask, and the ask is a specific time. You don't sell the website. You don't walk the demo. You set the call and hand over everything the closer needs to run it well.

How long

This is not a 30-second call

A good setting call runs 5 to 12 minutes. The owners who book and show are the ones who talked about their business with you first. Rushing to the ask is the number one reason calls die.

  • 0:00 – 0:30Open & pattern interrupt
  • 0:30 – 6:00Discovery: business, online presence, investment, 1–10
  • 6:00 – 7:00Reason for the call: the fit call
  • As neededHandle the objection live
  • 7:00 – 10:00Set it, lock it, confirm the invite
  • After hang-upSlack hand-off and GHL notes, within 5 minutes
The golden rule

Treat every owner who picks up as a real client

Don't decide for them that they can't afford it, won't care, or aren't serious. Your tone decides theirs.

Do

Talk to them like a business owner you respect. Curious, calm, a little informal. You already did work for them. Act like it.

Don't

Pre-qualify them out in your head because the shop looks small or the owner sounds rushed. Some of the best clients sounded the busiest.

(02) The appointment

What you're setting them onto

The call

Demo walkthrough & fit call

Length

15–30 minutes, on Zoom or phone, booked through Calendly.

With

The closer: the person who actually builds the site. Not a salesperson reading a script.

What happens

They see the demo built for their business, on screen. The closer fixes anything that's off and answers every question.

The point

Decide together whether Vanguard is the right fit. If it is, the site goes live within 14 days of kick-off.

How to describe it

Say it the same way every time

"It's a quick call with [Closer], who builds these. They'll walk you through the site we made for [Business Name], tweak anything that's off, and you can both see if it makes sense to go live with it. If it's not for you, no problem at all.""Fit" is the frame. They're not agreeing to buy. They're agreeing to look and decide.

(03) Segments

Who you're calling

Check the GHL stage first

Four kinds of lead, four openers

A · No website

Only a Google listing or Facebook page. Your strongest lead: the demo is the first site they've ever seen for their business.

B · Weak website

A site exists but it's slow, broken on mobile, outdated, or has no way to book. Lead with the specific gap, never an insult.

C · Callback

They asked you to call back, or replied to an email. They know who you are. Skip the interrupt, go straight to "picking up where we left off."

D · No-show

Booked, didn't show. Call within 15 minutes of the missed slot. No guilt, just rebook.

(04) Prep · 60 seconds per lead

Before you dial

Every lead, every time

If any box is empty, don't dial yet

  1. Compliance check

    Number scrubbed against the National Do Not Call Registry and our internal do-not-call list. It's between 8am and 9pm in their time zone.

  2. Demo is live

    Open the demo link and check it on your phone. If it's broken or still has a [TBD], don't call. Tell the owner on Slack.

  3. Names

    Business name, owner's first name if we have it, and how to say both. If there's no owner name, plan to ask for "whoever handles the marketing."

  4. One true, specific detail

    Their rating and review count, a service they're known for, the gap on their site. Something real you can say in the first 30 seconds.

  5. Calendly open

    The closer's calendar open in a tab, with two slots in the next 48 hours already picked out.

  6. Right time of day

    Don't call a salon at 11am on a Saturday or a restaurant at 6pm. Check the industry playbook for when the owner actually picks up.

(05) The live call

Seven steps, in order

Step 1 · Open & pattern interrupt

Name, honesty, the demo, then a question

Every cold call sounds the same in the first five seconds. Break the pattern by being honest that it's a cold call, then give them a reason to stay: something already built for them.

"Hey, is this [First Name]? … [First Name], it's [Your Name] from Vanguard. I'll be honest, this is a cold call. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling, and you tell me if it's worth more?"Wait for the yes. Almost everyone says "go ahead." Then use the segment opener below.

A · No website

"I was looking up [niche] in [city] and found [Business Name]: [4.8 stars, 120 reviews], which is great. But there's no website, just the [Google listing]. So we actually went ahead and built you one, a demo using your services and info. I'm calling to see if you'd want to see it."

B · Weak website

"I was on your site and noticed [there's no way to book online / it's hard to use on a phone]. We built a demo of what an updated version could look like, using your services. I wanted to see if you'd want a look."

C · Callback

"Hey [First Name], it's [Your Name] from Vanguard. You told me to try you [today / this week] about the site we built for [Business Name]. Is now still OK?"

D · No-show

"Hey [First Name], it's [Your Name] from Vanguard. We had you down with [Closer] at [time] to walk through your site. Totally get it, things come up. Want to grab a new time? I've got [slot] or [slot]."

If you get the front desk, use Training Module 01. Be honest about who you are; never pretend to be a customer or someone they know.

Step 2 · Discovery, go deep

Get them talking about their business. Ask, then shut up.

Discovery is where calls are won. The owner should talk more than you. Ask one question, let them finish, and ask a follow-up on what they just said. Write down their exact words; the closer will use them.

A · The business & where they want it to go

  • Where customers come from"How do most new customers find you right now?"
  • What they want more of"If you could fill your calendar with one kind of job, what would it be?"
  • Capacity"If ten more of those came in next month, could you handle them?"
  • Go deeper"Why that one?" · "What would that change for you?"

B · Their online presence right now

  • The search test"When someone Googles [Business Name], what do they find?"
  • History"Have you ever had a website, or tried to build one? What happened?"
  • Who handles it"Who looks after that stuff now, you or someone else?"
  • Lost business"Ever had someone say they couldn't find you, or went to a competitor who had a site?"

C · Investment fit & who decides

  • Past spend"Have you put money into marketing before: ads, a site, flyers, anything? How did it go?"
  • The honest ballpark"So nothing on the call surprises you: the demo is free, and if you go ahead, most of our builds land around two thousand, one-time. Is that something the business could do if it brought in more customers?"
  • Decision-maker"Is it just you who decides on something like this, or is there a partner who'd want to be on the call?"

The ballpark is the only number you ever say. No exact quotes, discounts or payment plans; the closer handles those. If they ask for an exact price, use Training Module 03.

D · Intent, the 1–10

"On a scale of 1 to 10, how important is it to get a proper website up for [Business Name] this year?"Whatever they say, follow with:

"What's keeping it from a 9 or a 10?"That answer is the real objection. Write it down word for word; it's the most useful thing you'll give the closer.

  • 8 – 10Book it. Go straight to Step 3.
  • 5 – 7Book it. Note what's holding them back, so the closer starts there.
  • 1 – 4Ask "What would need to change for it to matter?" If they still want to see the demo, book it. If not, thank them and set a follow-up date in GHL.
Step 3 · Reason for the call

Connect what they told you to the fit call

Use their words from discovery. Then position the call as the obvious next step, not a favour they're doing you.

"OK, so you want more [their words: e.g. 'bridal bookings'], and right now people Googling you only find [the Facebook page]. That's exactly what the demo was built around. Here's why I called: the next step is 20 minutes with [Closer], who built it. They'll show it to you on screen, change whatever's off, and you'll both know whether it's a fit. Fair?""Fair?" is a soft yes. Once they say it, move straight to setting the time.

Step 4 · Handle the objection live

Acknowledge, answer briefly, point back to the call

Most objections are questions the fit call exists to answer. Don't try to win the argument on the phone. Use "the ledge" ("Totally fair") and bring it back to the call.

  • "How much is it?""Fair question. It depends on what you need, which is what the call figures out. Most of our builds land around two thousand, one-time, and the demo itself is free."
  • "We already have a website.""Yeah, I saw it. That's why I called: [specific gap]. The demo fixes that. Worth 20 minutes to see if it's better?"
  • "Just email it to me.""I can. Honestly though, it lands better when [Closer] walks you through it and changes things live. Can we do [slot]? I'll email you the confirmation right now."
  • "I need to talk to my partner.""Makes total sense. Let's get them on the call too, so you both see it at once. What time works for both of you?"
  • "I'm too busy right now.""Totally get it, that's usually a good sign. When does it slow down? Even early next week, before you open?"
  • "What's the catch?""No catch. We build demos so owners can see it instead of imagining it. If you like it, the call is where you hear how it works. If you don't, you lose 20 minutes."
  • "How'd you get my number?""It's listed publicly on your Google profile. That's how we found [Business Name]. Is this still the best number?"
  • "Not interested.""Totally fair. Can I ask, is it the timing or the website itself?" If it's a firm no, thank them and stop. If they say "don't call me," add them to the do-not-call list today.
Step 5 · Set the call

Assume it, two slots, lock the details, book it live

  1. Assume it

    "Let's get you in with [Closer]."Not "would you be interested in…". You're scheduling, not asking permission.

  2. Offer two specific slots

    "I've got Tuesday at 11 or Wednesday at 3, your time. Which works better?"Two options, both in the next 48 hours. Never "when are you free?"

  3. Lock the details

    "What's the best email for the invite? … And is [number] the best number for [Closer] to reach you?"Read the email back letter by letter.

  4. Book it live in Calendly

    While they're still on the line. Put their exact words from discovery in the booking notes.

  5. Confirm they got it

    "I just sent it. Can you check your email? It should say 'Vanguard' and [day, time]."Wait until they say they see it. If it's not there, check spam together or fix the email.

  6. Ask for texts, the right way

    "Is this a cell I can text a reminder to?"Only a clear yes counts. Log "SMS consent: yes, [date/time], verbal" in GHL. No yes means email only.

  7. Get the commitment

    "[Closer] blocks that time just for you. If anything comes up, you'll let me know so we can move it, yeah?"A spoken yes here is what turns a booking into a show.

Step 6 · Introduce the closer

Set up the Slack hand-off and frame it on the call

The booking posts to #bookings on Slack automatically from GHL. Before the call ends, tell the owner what happens next, so the closer's first message isn't from a stranger.

"You'll get a quick note from [Closer] today with the demo link, so you can have a look beforehand if you want. Then on [day] they'll walk you through it."Promise only what will happen. If you're not sure the closer will message today, say "before the call."

In Slack

Reply in the thread under the booking alert with the hand-off note (section 08). Tag the closer.

Hot lead?

If they're an 8–10 or asked for something specific, message the closer directly as well.

Step 7 · Lock the show & hand off

The last 30 seconds, then 5 minutes of notes

"Can you pop it in your calendar now, while we're on? … Perfect. Anything you want [Closer] to look at before the call?"Their answer goes straight into the hand-off note.

  1. Move the opportunity in GHL

    Stage to Booked, yourself as Setter Assigned. No Setter Assigned means no commission.

  2. Write the hand-off note

    In GHL notes and the Slack thread, within 5 minutes of hanging up, while you still remember their words.

  3. Day-before check

    If they agreed to texts, the reminder goes out automatically. If not, the email reminder does. Don't send anything extra by text.

(06) No answer

If they don't pick up

Cadence

Different times of day, up to six attempts over two weeks

  • Attempt 1 · Day 1Call. Leave the 25-second voicemail. Send the "Demo site for [Business Name]" email the same day.
  • Attempt 2 · Day 2Call at a different time: if you tried mid-morning, try just before they open or around closing.
  • Attempt 3 · Day 4Call. No voicemail. Send the day 3–5 follow-up email.
  • Attempt 4 · Day 6Call at lunch or early afternoon.
  • Attempt 5 · Day 8Call. Send the final "Closing the loop" email.
  • Attempt 6 · Day 12Last call, with a short voicemail. Then move the lead to Nurture in GHL.
Channel rules
  • Phone, voicemail and email only. A Facebook or Instagram DM is allowed using the templates in the Setter Script.
  • No texts to anyone who hasn't said yes to texts on a call.
  • Only call between 8am and 9pm in their time zone.
  • Anyone who says "stop calling" goes on the do-not-call list the same day. No further attempts.
(07) Lines you don't cross

What setters do not do

Non-negotiable

Break one of these and the deal pays no commission

Never
  • Quote an exact price, a discount or a payment plan. The two-thousand ballpark is the only number you say.
  • Walk them through the demo or sell the website. That's the closer's job.
  • Promise results: rankings, a number of customers, revenue.
  • Invent stats, testimonials or client names. Only use what's in the Setter Playbook.
  • Pretend to be a customer, a friend or someone they've worked with, to get past the front desk.
  • Text anyone who hasn't said yes to texts, or call anyone on a do-not-call list.
  • Ask for passwords, logins or payment details.
  • Talk down their current site or a competitor.
  • Book a call with someone who can't make the decision, without inviting the person who can.
  • Leave a call booked without a confirmed email, a confirmed time and a hand-off note.
